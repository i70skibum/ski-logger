.DISCUSSION /Through Sunday/...
Issued at 1129 PM MST Sun Nov 15 2026

A mix of stratus, mist, and fog are evident across the eastern
plains tonight. Based on surface observations, most of the lower
visibilities seem to be east of the main stratus deck that has
persisted across Lincoln county into the far northeastern corner
this evening. Short term guidance continues to develop additional
fog across most of the Platte River valley into the
northern/northwestern Denver metro, though recent HRRR runs have
backed off of the southwestern extent of this fog through Monday
AM. We've kept patchy fog in the grids across most of the lower
elevations but did trim some of it out of the I-25 corridor. 

Monday should be a mild day across most of our forecast area as
the ridge axis shifts eastward. We'll have broad but dry
southwesterly flow aloft during the day ahead of our next
shortwave, with temperatures again reaching the low to mid 60s
across the plains. Similar conditions are expected on Tuesday as
the shortwave continues to approach the region and the mid-level
flow strengthens in response. Highs are likely to reach the mid
60s in the metro with some guidance hinting at potentially warmer
temperatures.

The first of two storm systems will reach the area on Wednesday,
though this one looks to only provide a glancing blow to the
region as it quickly ejects into the northern Rockies/northern
Great Plains. Guidance does have a decent push of moisture across
the western half of the state Tuesday night, but quickly dries us
out as the mid-level low pushes into northeastern Montana during 
the day Wednesday. While the 700-500mb flow briefly is favorable 
for orographic snow showers, the limited duration of sufficient 
moisture will keep snow accumulations to a minimum (generally an 
inch or less). The main story this period may be wind, as this 
looks to be our first bora type event of the season. Cross 
sections reveal a brief/marginally favorable mountain wave 
amplification setup Wednesday morning. I am a bit skeptical of 
the higher end solution advertised by the RRFS at this time, but 
50kt cross barrier flow along with a modest stable layer would 
support a few gusts near high wind criteria over the Front Range 
mountains and higher foothills. On the other hand, any additional 
northward shift with this shortwave would likely limit wind 
potential across our forecast area.

There should be a brief break between systems on Thursday, with
mild and generally quiet weather across the state. The next system
arrives Friday and looks a little more organized and longer 
lasting, as a broad trough axis moves through the region. While 
this is still unlikely to produce much (other than maybe some 
gusty winds) across the plains, the overall setup does look more 
favorable for mountain snow across the northern half of the state.
Guidance generally features a longer period of orographic snow, 
initially supported by some QG ascent and a favorable jet 
position. Chances for snow should linger into Saturday before
moisture diminishes on Sunday.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Tuesday/...
Issued at 1053 AM MST Mon Nov 16 2026

VFR conditions expected to prevail through the TAF period. Winds are 
light and variable and are expected to stay that way for the next 
few hours before settling in easterly flow for most of the afternoon 
and evening. Around 02Z, winds should start turning southeasterly, 
then become south/southwesterly overnight. Patchy fog could develop 
over the eastern plains again tomorrow morning, though the 
predominantly southerly winds expected across the area would keep 
any fog to the east/northeast of the TAF sites. That being said, if 
KDEN or KBJC sees any northerly winds in the early morning hours, 
they could experience fog.

There is good agreement in the models for a Denver cyclone to start 
developing Tuesday morning around 18/19Z and strengthening through 
the afternoon. Current guidance places the center of the low 
slightly to the west/northwest of KDEN, with increasing chances for 
gusty (20-30kts) southwesterly winds over both KDEN and KAPA. Should 
this solution pan out, KBJC would not experience these gusty winds. 
Ultimately, both the wind direction and speed tomorrow afternoon 
will depend on where the center of the cyclone develops.
