.DISCUSSION /Through Friday/...
Issued at 1246 PM MST Fri Nov 20 2026

A strong southwest jet aloft has brought gusty winds to the high 
country and the majority of the plains today. Gusty winds are likely 
to continue for the next few hours. The high country could see wind 
gusts of 50-60 mph with slightly higher gusts possible at the 
highest elevations. We expect 30-40 mph wind gusts to continue 
across the plains. These winds are expected to slack off in the next 
few hours as the upper-level flow starts to weaken gradually, but 
some gusty winds could be possible through late tonight at the 
highest elevations. Additionally, relative humidity remains at or 
below 20% for most of the plains south of I-76. Thus, we have had 
some extreme to near-critical fire weather conditions today and this 
threat will continue for a few more hours. Today is forecast to be 
the warmest day of the upcoming week with highs mainly in the 60s 
for the plains. Cooler air is starting to filter into the northern 
plains and northern metro at this hour.

Current radar shows some snow showers ongoing across the central and 
northern high country with visibilities briefly dropping. There 
is the chance for some light snow accumulations this afternoon 
for the high country with the Park Range being the most favored 
location. Only about an additional inch or two of snow seems 
plausible as the shortwave departs. 

A cold front is progged to move through the area late this afternoon 
from north to south. This will usher in cooler temperatures for the 
week ahead. Saturday morning, we expect an increase in QG ascent as 
the trough moves through and moisture has slightly increased in the 
latest modeling. We have increased cloud cover for tomorrow, which 
could keep temperatures across the urban corridor right at or just 
above freezing. On Saturday morning into the early afternoon, snow 
levels drop as cold air advection continues, 700 mb temperatures 
cool, and weak upslope flow is expected. There is a low chance 
(~20%) for some light snow on the plains with about a 30% chance for 
the southern foothills. For the urban corridor, in particular, there 
is only about a 10% chance for very light accumulations given the 
marginal temperatures. Odds increase very slightly for the Palmer 
Divide. The central and southern high country have a higher chance 
of seeing some accumulations tomorrow morning, but only around 
one inch. If the ingredients can come together, some locations in 
the southern foothills and southern high country could get a bit 
more accumulation.

Saturday will be much cooler than today with temperatures struggling 
to reach the mid 40s for the plains. Ridging quickly moves in 
Saturday night into Sunday, but highs are forecast to only 
moderate a few degrees on Sunday afternoon. Models and ensemble 
guidance continue to show the potential for two different systems 
affecting our area next week- one at the beginning of the week and
one towards the end of the week. Run-to-run and model-to-model 
variability remains high, so there is very low confidence in any 
precipitation timing/impacts. More westerly/northwesterly flow 
aloft means our central and southern high country are most 
favored, in general. Monday into early Tuesday brings the next 
chance for snow for the high country. The NBM does a good job of 
depicting that only light accumulations are expected. Some light 
rain and/or snow could spread onto the plains on Monday night into
Tuesday morning.

We are confident that the plains will see highs in the 40s to near 
50 and lows in the low/mid 20s which will continue through most of 
next week. These temperatures will be 5 to even 10 degrees below 
average- the coolest weather of the season. This forecast will be 
something to keep tabs on with the busy travel week ahead.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Saturday/...
Issued at 1207 PM MST Fri Nov 20 2026

Breezy sustained SW winds of 15-20 kts with gusts to around 30 kts
will prevail at KDEN and KAPA for much of the afternoon. While
gusts at times could exceed 30 kts, it is unlikely they exceed 35
kts. There is more uncertainty in the wind forecast for KBJC as 
they tend to be sheltered from these SW winds. Recent obs indicate
a more northerly direction, but it is still about a 60% chance
winds shift to the SW rather than a 40% chance to remain N or NW.  
A more westerly flow will occur late this afternoon into the 
evening along with winds speeds weakening after sunset. Tonight 
KDEN and KAPA will favor a SE flow before turning SSW for 
Saturday afternoon. Expect KBJC to shift to NW/W drainage flow and
SE flow to develop tomorrow.

Cloud cover should also increase tonight with BKN layers 
developing near 030-050 and overcast conditions above. Cloud are
likely to lift higher as the day continues Saturday. There is 
~20% chance a snow shower could develop near KBJC or KAPA, but no
visibility reductions are expected.
