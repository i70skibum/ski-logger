.DISCUSSION /Through Sunday/...
Issued at 113 PM MST Sun Nov 15 2026

Observations show skies starting to clear up over the foothills and 
urban corridor, with some cloud cover lingering over the eastern 
plains. Temperatures should start warming up as clouds continue to 
lift, with highs expected to be in the 50s for most of the 
plains/urban corridor and 40s in the mountains. Areas where cloud 
cover is expected to stick around longer (mainly Lincoln County and 
southern Elbert County) will stay cooler, with highs in the upper 
40s/low 50s.

Fog development is expected again tomorrow morning over the eastern 
plains and pushing into the urban corridor. Highest confidence for 
visibility less than 2SM is over Lincoln County and southern Weld 
County into Morgan County, though isolated visibility reductions 
over the plains and the Denver metro area can't be ruled out. Once 
fog/low clouds lift, temperatures will warm back up and stay warmer 
than average through Tuesday thanks to an upper-level ridge moving 
east across Colorado. Highs will climb into the mid 50s in the 
mountain valleys and the low 60s across the plains & urban corridor 
both days.

Things get a bit more interesting on Wednesday, with a shortwave 
entering the area and bringing rain/snow showers to the 
mountains. At the start of the system late Tuesday night, 
elevations above 8500ft will see snow, with lower valleys seeing 
rain or a rain/snow mix. This should transition to all snow as the
shortwave moves eastward Wednesday morning. Accumulations are 
expected to be low, only a trace to a couple hundredths, though 
the highest peaks could see up to an inch. With the progression of
the shortwave, cross-barrier westerly flow aloft and strong 
subsidence could create favorable conditions for a high wind event
for the Front Range Wednesday morning. The current forecast has 
wind gusts reaching 60-70mph at the highest peaks, with more 
widespread gusts of 35-45mph along the Front Range. Multiple 
models have also started indicating the potential for a stable 
layer between 700-500mb, which, coupled with the cross-barrier 
flow and subsidence, could be a favorable environment for a 
mountain wave. We'll be watching this and the other ingredients 
for high wind/mountain wave events as we get closer to Wednesday 
morning. 

Thursday should be cool and dry behind the shortwave, with the next 
system moving in over the weekend. Recent runs favor the more open 
trough solution, with the bulk of precipitation staying west of our 
CWA. Ensembles continue to trend downward for precipitation chances 
over the plains, though the mountains could still see some snow. 
Mountain snow could start late Thursday and continue through mid 
Saturday, and for now the best chance for precip over the plains 
looks to be late Friday. At this time, the chance for snow 
accumulations over the plains is low, about 10% chance or less for a 
trace.

.AVIATION /06Z TAFS through 06Z Tuesday/...
Issued at 1051 PM MST Sun Nov 15 2026

Low clouds and fog are expected to form again across eastern 
Colorado overnight and into Monday morning. The fog will come 
close to reaching DEN, but if southwest winds persist as expected,
it should remain just north to northeast of the airport. Will 
have VCFG in the TAF for DEN, but not in the prevailing or a tempo
group. At BJC and APA, fog chances are lower, but haze could drop
visibility to 3 to 5 miles toward 12Z Monday. However confidence 
(20-30% chance) in the haze is not high enough at this time to add
it to the TAF. Winds are expected to be light, less than 10 knots
through the TAF period and follow a typical diurnal direction 
pattern.
