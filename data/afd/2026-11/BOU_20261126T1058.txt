.DISCUSSION /Through Wednesday/...
Issued at 1024 PM MST Wed Nov 25 2026

A chilly Thanksgiving morning with lows generally in the 20s 
across the plains will give way to above normal temperatures in 
the afternoon. Highs for Thanksgiving will be in the 50s to near 
60 for the plains and 30s to mid 40s for the mountains and 
valleys. Winds will remain on the lighter side with mostly sunny 
to partly cloudy skies for the holiday.

For the system this weekend, the GFS has trended closer to what 
the Euro (and a few other models) has been showing over the past 
few days. The increased model consensus has allowed our forecast 
confidence to increase for this weekend. Upper level northwest 
flow will strengthen on Friday, bringing breezy conditions to the 
higher terrain starting Friday morning. Wind gusts will be around 
40 to 65mph on Friday for elevations generally above 9000ft. We 
could see some stronger winds come off of the Cheyenne ridge and 
into our northern plains counties Friday afternoon as well, with 
gusts around 25 to 40mph. Downsloping northwest winds will allow 
temperatures to warm further, putting highs around 60 degrees for 
much of the plains on Friday.

An upper level shortwave will approach the area late Friday, moving 
through by late Saturday. Ahead of the shortwave, we'll see 
increasing moisture and decent QG lift. An upper level jet is also 
forecast to develop, with our area in the left exit region for 
much of Saturday. At the surface, a strong cold front will move 
through Saturday afternoon, bringing strong winds (gusting around 
30 to 55mph) and much cooler temperatures. A few models are showing
SBCAPE above 50J/kg on Saturday, which combined with the strong 
frontogenesis is causing some concern for banded snowfall and 
potentially even snow squalls. This and the potential for blowing
snow from the strong winds could lead to hazardous travel
conditions Saturday. The other thing we'll need to watch is the 
potential for anticyclonic turning of the winds as it comes off 
the Cheyenne Ridge. A slight eastward turning would increase the 
potential for higher snow amounts in the southern foothills, south
metro, and Palmer Divide area. 

The highest snow accumulations are forecast to be in the Park
Range with most likely liquid totals (25th to 75th percentiles) 
of 0.6 to 1.6 inches. With SLRs between 10 and 15, a Winter
Weather Advisory is likely once we get closer, with potential for
a Winter Storm Warning. The rest of our mountain areas are
currently forecast to see between 2 and 8 inches of snow with 
this event. For the plains, precipitation will generally be light 
with rain transitioning to snow behind the front. Snow 
accumulations are forecast to generally remain below 0.5 inches 
for the urban corridor and plains, with higher amounts for the 
Palmer and Foothills. 

This system will move off to the east late Saturday, leaving 
colder and drier conditions for Sunday. Lows Sunday morning in the
plains will be in the teens to 20s and highs will be in the 40s. 
Winds will remain breezy Sunday morning, putting apparent 
temperatures in the low teens to single digits for the eastern 
plains. An active pattern will continue through the middle of next
week. We could see a few more chances for mountain snow next week as
an upper level shortwave moves through Monday and our next bigger
system approaches Wednesday.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Friday/...
Issued at 1058 AM MST Thu Nov 26 2026

VFR will continue through the TAF period. Highclouds will 
increase with mostly cloudy skies tonight and Friday. Ceilings 
will remain above 10,000 feet. Light winds will continue today 
becoming northeast after 18Z. Southeast winds are expected around 
or shortly after 00Z, then turning to the southwest by 06Z. Flow 
aloft increases Friday ahead of an approaching storm system, 
bringing a period of gusty west-northwest winds up to 25 knots. 
The wind forecast will be more uncertain Friday, as models also 
show the potential for winds to turn anti-cyclonic and end up an 
east to northeast direction later in the day.
