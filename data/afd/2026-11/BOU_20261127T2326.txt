.DISCUSSION /Through Friday/...
Issued at 1108 PM MST Fri Nov 27 2026

Snow is ongoing across the Winter Storm Warning and Advisory 
areas as of late this evening. The northern Front Range zone, 
which includes Rocky Mountain NP, was upgraded to a Winter Storm 
Warning for Saturday. Looking at webcams, snow is already coming 
down with pretty decent rates and the best lift is yet to come. 
Lift will maximize in the early to mid morning to early afternoon
as the upper level trough approaches and the left exit region of 
the jet is over the area. At the same time, a strong cold front 
will be moving through the area. Models continue to trend higher 
with QPF or the mountains, and therefore snowfall amounts. As the
previous AFDs have mentioned, there is potential for snow squalls
this morning and afternoon (primarily along and following the 
front). The best chance for snow squalls today will be in the 
mountains, but we can't rule one out on the plains. Even if the 
plains and foothills don't see a snow squall, there is still a 
chance for banded precipitation this afternoon and early evening.
What will likely start as rain will transition to snow as 
temperatures drop behind the front. Areas that see heavy snow 
bands will experience significant decreases in visiblities and 
hazardous driving conditions. 

High res models are showing an anticyclonic turning of the winds at 
the surface to around 750 to 700mb near the southern foothills. This 
will allow for decent upslope in the south metro, southern 
foothills, and Palmer Divide area. Right now, we're keeping snow 
accumulations below Advisory criteria with models showing a low 
chance (<15%) this area exceeds the 4 inch Advisory threshold.
But, we still considered a Winter Weather Advisory for our 
southern foothills zone given the ingredients in place and the 
potential impacts if a heavy band does form there. For now though,
we've decided to hold off on the Winter Weather Advisory in this
area. For the metro, we are expecting rain to transition to snow 
behind the front, with the potential for some light accumulation 
on grasses. Road temperatures will be fairly warm, so unless a 
very heavy snow band develops over the area, roads should remain 
mostly clear on the plains. The main travel impact with any snow 
in the plains will be lowering visibilities. 

Snow will taper off in the evening as subsidence builds in behind 
the departing shortwave. Temperatures will remain cooler on Sunday, 
with highs in the 40s to near 50 across the plains. More zonal 
flow will set up on Monday, with a weak disturbance moving through
the flow aloft in the evening. We could see some light snow 
showers in the mountains as the disturbance approaches and moves 
through, but impacts should remain minimal. 

Our next system will develop off the West Coast late Tuesday. An 
upper level low is forecast to move over southern California and 
towards the four corners region Wednesday. The exact track of this 
system remains relatively uncertain and it will have a big impact on 
the potential for precip and snow on the plains. If the track ends 
up trending more south, our snow chances would decrease 
significantly. But if the track of the upper low is closer to what 
the current Euro is showing, we're looking at our next chance for
mountains and plains snow late Wednesday through Thursday. The 
current NBM has a medium chance (40%-60%) for at least a trace of 
snow south of I-76 with this event and around a 20% to 50% chance 
for at least an inch of snow for the metro and south. While 
there's still a lot of uncertainty with the track, these are the 
best probabilities for snow in the plains that I've seen so far 
this season.

.AVIATION /06Z TAFS through 06Z Sunday/...
Issued at 1042 PM MST Fri Nov 27 2026

VFR conditions expected tonight through early tomorrow morning.
Winds are currently gusting from the northwest at DEN and BJC as
channeled winds have continued tonight. However, expect drainage
flow to return in a few hours at DEN. BJC will likely be gusting
from the west/northwest all night. 

Saturday is still gearing up to be an impactful day due to a
system bringing strong winds and precipitation to all terminals. 
By 15Z-17Z, lee cyclogenesis is favored to develop, allowing gusty
westerly winds up to 20-28 kts for a few hours. A strong cold 
front will then race across the region by 20Z-22Z, which will 
transition winds to the north. Model guidance has been very 
consistent with gusts of 40-50 kts possible with the frontal 
passage. I have upped the wind gusts in the TAF to reflect that 
potential. After the quick burst of strong winds, northerly winds 
should weaken a bit, but expect northerly wind gusts of 20-30 kts 
through 03Z. Models are in good agreement of winds weakening
significantly by 05Z-07Z, with generally light (<10 kts) and
variable winds overnight. 

In terms of precipitation, all terminals have a chance to see
rain/snow showers with the frontal passage. The cold front will
drastically drop temperatures and any precipitation that does fall
during that time should transition from rain to snow. Snow
accumulations should be light (<0.5"), however convective snow
showers will likely reduce visibilities down to 1 - 1 1/2 SM at
times. Ceilings will lower with any precipitation, with guidance
favoring 500-1000 feet. A few low clouds may linger after 23Z-24Z,
but generally expect VFR conditions to a little after that.
