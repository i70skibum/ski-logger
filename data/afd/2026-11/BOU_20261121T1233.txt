.DISCUSSION /Through Saturday/...
Issued at 1222 PM MST Sat Nov 21 2026

Water vapor imagery shows a trough over Colorado with a strong upper 
level jet across the eastern half of the state. This jet and PVA 
from the trough are providing forcing for what has turned into a 
decent snow event for the foothills and western suburbs of the 
Denver metro. There were some reports of 3-5" of snow in the 
foothills with up to 2" in the western metro. This jet is still 
producing snow showers mainly in two areas. One area is in Morgan 
and eastern Weld Counties where the best low level frontogenesis in 
occurring. With the little bit of added elevation in northeastern
Weld County, some snow accumulation may have occurred and may 
continue this afternoon. The other area of snow is mainly across 
the southern foothills and South Park. It's possible some areas 
near Westcreek receive 1-3" of snow from the snow showers this 
afternoon mainly on grassy surfaces. PoPs were increased for both 
of those areas this afternoon. In the Denver metro, there appears 
to be too much dry air for any snow showers to form this 
afternoon. Otherwise, there will be isolated convective snow 
showers this afternoon over the higher terrain as some instability
builds in. 

Tonight, skies will clear as the trough moves past Colorado. Light 
northeast winds will advect moisture from the Northern Plains into 
Colorado. There may be some patchy fog that develops and with 
temperatures below freezing, it could create some slick spots on the 
roadways. 

On Sunday, weak ridging will be overhead which will keep conditions 
dry. Temperatures will remain cool with high clouds and generally 
light winds. 

On Monday, a shortwave trough will move over Colorado. The main PVA 
forcing from this trough will be split around Colorado with a 
wave over New Mexico and another in Montana. Lee cyclogenesis 
will develop across northeast Colorado which will attempt to bring
in moisture but there will remain very limited moisture with this
system. Due to the weak forcing and limited moisture, the plains 
are expected to remain dry. The I-70 corridor mountains, Park 
County, and the southern foothills will have enough instability 
that develops that snow showers will likely (70% chance) form. 
Despite the high chance of snow, the snow amounts will be 
minimal. Generally less than 2" of snow is expected in the 
mountains with light impacts. 

The back edge of the trough will move east of Colorado Tuesday 
afternoon. Most ensemble members are showing ridging generally over 
Colorado and the Southwest US starting Wednesday and lasting 
through the weekend. However, a few (roughly 10-20%) have a trough
moving close to Colorado out of northwest flow that could produce
light precipitation mainly in the mountains of Colorado Thursday 
into Friday. Therefore, the forecast calls for a dry Thanksgiving 
in Denver and across the plains with a chance of light snow in the
mountains. Overall, no major travel impacts are expected for the 
Thanksgiving travel period.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Sunday/...
Issued at 1040 AM MST Sat Nov 21 2026

With the dissipation of snow showers on radar, no more impacts 
are expected. Ceilings have already began to rise and VFR will 
prevail for the rest of the afternoon and evening. Heading into 
Sunday morning, there is uncertainty regarding the potential for 
fog. The current likelihood is for fog to be in the vicinity of 
the airport with low stratus clouds for a few hours. However, a 
cyclone could develop tomorrow morning and this will heavily 
influence where the fog rolls in. After the morning fog potential,
VFR conditions will prevail. 

As for winds, expect light SSW winds the remainder of the
afternoon for KDEN and KAPA while KBJC should be a touch more
S/SE. Light and variable winds develop towards sunset before
shifting later in the night to a southeasterly flow. Sunday's
pattern will likely favor a light S/SE direction in the 
afternoon.
