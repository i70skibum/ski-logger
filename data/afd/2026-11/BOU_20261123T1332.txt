.DISCUSSION /Through Monday/...
Issued at 1245 PM MST Mon Nov 23 2026

It isn't often the case that the lowest confidence forecast period
is the near term (next 12-24 hours). However, this is the case
today as a weak trough drifts across the state through tonight.

There is considerable uncertainty in how this afternoon/evening's
mountain snow showers play out. While CAMs were very bullish with
precipitation potential as recently as the 12z cycle, there has
been a notable downtrend since then. Regional/global 
deterministic guidance hasn't not changed significantly, and 
supports at least light snow across the mountains beginning in a 
few hours. One may be wondering, "why the Winter Weather 
Advisory?" Today's forecast and advisory are almost entirely 
driven by favorable ingredients for heavy snow showers that could 
have travel impacts despite relatively light snowfall amounts in 
the grids. Very steep lapse rates (close to 8-9C/km through the 
lowest 6km) and modest mid- level convergence/lift, combined with 
efficient snow ratios could lead to brief periods of 1-2"/hr 
snowfall rates. It's possible that these snow showers stick 
mainly to the Mosquito Range, but any snow showers that reach the 
I-70 corridor could lead to brief but difficult travel conditions 
through this evening. Essentially this an impacts-driven advisory 
with a low floor but high ceiling in terms of travel impacts 
through the early overnight hours.

We still look on track to enter a period of mild and dry weather
for the rest of the week. A weak ridge is expected to develop to
our west on Tuesday, leading to a drier northwesterly flow aloft.
The ridge should slowly shift eastward and flatten by mid-week
while a strong cutoff low develops over the Ohio River valley.
Temperatures on Tuesday and Wednesday should return to the upper
40s to low 50s across the plains. Mid to upper 50s are likely for
Thanksgiving, with additional warming possible to end the week.

Forecast confidence decreases on Friday and Saturday as a broad
swath of moisture embedded in west-northwesterly flow moves 
towards the forecast area. This moisture is embedded in an area of
stronger flow aloft, located between a broad trough in south 
central Canada and a strong ridge to the west of Baja California. 
There has been poor consistency (both model to model and 
d[model]/dt) in this period, with the ECMWF generally further 
south with the jet and accompanying moisture/lift, while the GFS 
is on the drier side. This is also reflected across their 
respective ensemble distributions. Interestingly, the AIGEFS has 
shifted notably away from the physics-based GFS/GEFS today, though
it's not clear if this is an actual trend or run-to-run noise. I 
have a slight preference towards the wetter (snowier) solutions 
that would likely bring at least some minor travel impacts to the 
mountains for the start of the weekend.

Forecast confidence remains very low by the end of the forecast
period. Guidance generally keeps a very active subtropical jet
near the region, with another chance of mountain snow early next
week.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Tuesday/...
Issued at 1028 AM MST Mon Nov 23 2026

Light northeast winds are expected throughout the day today due 
to lee cyclogenesis over northeast Colorado. VFR conditions are 
expected through this evening. 

With a relatively cool and somewhat moist airmass in place across
northeast Colorado, there will be a chance of fog at all terminals
tonight. At this moment, it appears winds will be too light to
advect the low clouds/fog over the northeast corner of Colorado
into the Denver metro. So the chance for fog of 1/4 SM visibility
is roughly 20-30% at all terminals. A FEW001 group was added at
all terminals to give a heads up about fog potential. The most
likely time for fog would be between 10-15Z. 

Otherwise, winds will be light tomorrow with southeast winds
developing in the afternoon.
