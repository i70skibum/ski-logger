.DISCUSSION /Through Saturday/...
Issued at 1158 PM MST Sat Nov 14 2026

Fog has been slow to make progress in the last couple of hours, with 
observations showing an area of fog over Lincoln County, and another 
area of fog spilling over the CO-WY border over northernmost Weld 
County. At this time, a Dense Fog Advisory remains in effect for 
Lincoln County until 10 am Sunday morning. Visibilities could drop 
to <1/4 mile at times in the advisory area and adjacent plains. The 
advisory may need to be expanded if dense fog develops elsewhere. 
While we still expect fog to increase in coverage closer to sunrise, 
low clouds may limit the duration and extent of the lower (<1/4 mi) 
visibilities. 

How much temperatures warm up Sunday afternoon will be heavily 
dependent on the erosion (or lack thereof) of fog and/or low clouds. 
Given the trends in model guidance and was has been observed over 
the last couple of days, fog may be slow to erode over the 
southeastern portion of our CWA, with scattered low clouds lingering 
into the afternoon. As such, high temperatures over areas affected 
by the stubborn low clouds (mainly Lincoln County) may run 5-10 
degrees cooler than areas with direct sunshine Sunday afternoon. 
Residual moisture west of the Continental Divide may also lead to 
low clouds persisting through the late morning for our western 
counties. 

An upper level ridge to our west will continue to strengthen and 
drift east over our area on Monday, bringing us temperatures that 
are about 10 degrees warmer than climatology. Afternoon temperatures 
on Monday and Tuesday will climb into the low 60s for the Denver 
metro and east CO plains, with most mountain valleys warming up into 
the 50s each day as well. Flow aloft turns southerly and strengthens 
ahead of the next system on Tuesday afternoon, although the 
strongest winds now appear to remain just east of our area.

A weakening shortwave move over Colorado on Wednesday, and this will 
be our next chance for precipitation. As the previous AFD stated, 
this shortwave will move rather quick, only allowing for a few hours 
of light snow showers at the high elevations. Given the quick nature 
of this system, only a trace of snow accumulation is expected, with 
amounts up to 1-2 inches at the highest peaks >12kft. Cross-barrier 
westerly flow aloft on the backside of this disturbance will also 
encourage a period of downsloping winds late Wednesday morning. Wind 
gusts up to 35 mph could be possible for mountains valleys, with 
gusts as high as 55 mph for ridgelines and areas above the tree 
line. At low elevations, a cold front will push through the northern 
Colorado plains and Denver metro sometime late Wednesday with breezy 
northerly winds behind it. Slightly cooler temperatures should 
arrive behind this cold front, although temperatures are still 
likely to remain a couple of degrees above average. 

Unfortunately for snow lovers, both global ensembles and 
deterministic runs have trended downward in snow amounts with the 
system for next weekend. What was previously a potent cutoff low 
is now being depicted as a much weaker, open trough. This weaker 
solution would only suggest light mountain snow at best Friday 
into the weekend. However, it is worth noting that some ensemble 
members still bring at least one inch of snow to the Denver metro
sometime next weekend. One possible reason for the whiplash in 
solutions seems to be that global models keep struggling with the
location of cyclogenesis upstream off the northern Pacific Coast.
As has become evident over the last few days, there is a 
considerable uncertainty in the forecast beyond day 4, and the 
forecast could still shift in either direction. Decent agreement
still exists in temperatures cooling down to seasonal averages 
for the weekend.
