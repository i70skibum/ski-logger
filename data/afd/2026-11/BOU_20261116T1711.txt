.DISCUSSION /Through Monday/...
Issued at 220 PM MST Mon Nov 16 2026

GOES-19 satellite imagery shows mostly clear skies this afternoon 
after dense fog this morning across northeast Colorado. Today and 
Tuesday will be dry for the area and mild with ridging aloft. High 
temperatures on the plains are forecast to be in the low to mid 60s. 
High-resolution guidance is in good agreement that a Denver Cyclone 
is likely to form on Tuesday. As a result, some locations on the 
eastern plains could see wind gusts of 25-35 mph tomorrow afternoon. 
As the week progresses, high temperatures on the plains will cool 
down into the 50s. By late Tuesday evening into early Wednesday 
morning, troughing should move into the state. This will open the 
door for snow chances in the high country. Most locations will only 
see a dusting at most with limited moisture in place, but the Park 
Range and northern Gore Range could get up to 2 inches of snow given 
favorable upslope.

By early Wednesday morning as any precipitation begins to wind down, 
the ingredients for a Bora event could come together for our area. 
Cold air advection, little wind shear, and strong near-westerly 
cross barrier flow is expected to develop with mixed layer winds 
reaching or even exceeding 50-60 kts through much of Wednesday 
morning and into the early afternoon. On Wednesday morning, models 
show northeast CO near the right exit region of a jet aloft as well 
as strong subsidence which would aid in increasing wind speeds. 
Model soundings from the latest RRFS runs have shown a stable layer 
around 650-700 mb, so this could be something to watch for in terms 
of any enhancement of winds. Some potential limiting factors include 
only a very weak surface pressure gradient and the jet positioning. 
By Wednesday afternoon, the jet could be overhead, which would 
likely confine the strong wind gusts to far northern portions of our 
area and into Wyoming. 

REFS and NBM mean max wind gusts on Wednesday now show peak wind 
gusts of at least 60-65 mph for the northern Front Range mountains 
and 50-60 mph for the northern Front Range foothills. The high 
resolution guidance that has come in today is hinting at the 
potential for a few stronger wind gusts to make it down into the 
typical windy areas in these setups along the Front Range such as 
the Hwy 93 corridor as well as northern Weld County. This solution 
is still rather uncertain, but as of now we expect wind gusts to 
stay below high wind criteria for the Hwy 93 corridor. 

As is typical in these setups, there could be some narrow channels 
of 30-40 mph wind gusts for the Denver metro if the ingredients can 
come together. In general, confidence is increasing in the potential 
for a high wind event for portions of the high country north of I-70 
including the higher terrain of Larimer and Boulder counties on 
Wednesday morning and potentially lasting into the afternoon. 
Therefore, we have issued a High Wind Watch for the northern
Front Range foothills from 2am to 2pm Wednesday. The High Wind
Watch could need to be expanded in future packages if confidence 
increases in the peak wind gusts meeting criteria at the highest 
elevations.

On Thursday, we expect some weak ridging with near-average 
temperatures. The next system looks to move in starting Friday, but 
should progress slower than the early week system. Once again, 
this system will favor the high country for any precipitation with
southwest to west flow aloft. Precipitation chances will likely 
continue on Saturday. Ensemble guidance shows that the plains has 
a very low chance for some light scattered rain from this system. 
At this time, snow chances remain near zero for Denver through at 
least this weekend. Therefore, we are nearly guaranteed to have a 
top 5 latest first snow for Denver. With another strong jet aloft 
moving through and a tightening pressure gradient, we could be in 
for another period of stronger wind gusts Friday afternoon into 
Saturday. From Sunday onward, a more zonal pattern looks to be in 
place with signs in ensemble guidance of near-average to below 
average temperatures becoming more likely.

.AVIATION /00Z TAFS through 00Z Wednesday/...
Issued at 509 PM MST Mon Nov 16 2026

Current observations show a weak cyclone positioned southeast of 
Denver, and a boundary moving in from the east with it. Because 
of this cyclone, winds at KAPA are currently NNW, and KDEN is 
experiencing a shift to northeast winds. We expect winds at KDEN 
to continue shifting around to the NNW with the cyclone for a
brief period before SSW drainage winds become established by 
~03Z. Wind speeds with this cyclone are quite light, under 08KT. 
Fog is expected to develop north of Denver just outside or right 
at the vicinity between 10Z and 15Z, so we have opted to add a 
VCFG for KDEN.

The main concern for tomorrow afternoon will be the development of a 
cyclone somewhere over the southeast Denver metro, which brings 
uncertainty to the wind forecast. We expect gusty SSW winds to reach 
KAPA and KDEN starting at ~19-20Z, with gusts up to 30KT possible. 
As the cyclone tracks to the east, KDEN could experience a brief 
period of northwest winds closer to sunset (~00Z). Winds at KBJC 
should be light and easterly initially, with a shift to the north 
late in the afternoon. However, the exact location and evolution of 
this cyclone could greatly alter wind direction at the three 
airports.
