.DISCUSSION /Through Friday/...
Issued at 1200 AM MST Sat Nov 21 2026

The positively tilted upper level trough over the Great Basin 
continues to slide east this morning, weakening as it tracks over
Colorado Saturday afternoon. The southwest jet ahead of this wave
which was responsible for the widespread gusty winds Friday 
afternoon also begins to weaken and become more southerly. As 
previous AFDs have stated, this system will bring another round of
snow showers to our mountains, this time favoring the central 
mountains and southern Front Range. We have increased snow 
accumulations a tad to better reflect recent trends, with totals 
now ranging between 1.0 and 2.0 inches. 

The chance for some snowflakes falling over the Denver metro still 
exists, and as the finer details become more clear, there are a 
few features that we have been examining. First, although the 
trough passing over the region looks quite weak, it will still 
bring some favorable ingredients for synoptic ascent. One of 
these ingredients would be the position of the right-entrance 
region of an embedded jet streak, favoring ascent over the Denver 
metro sometime Saturday morning. Other features that would promote
and/or indicate QG ascent would be the passage of the remaining 
PV anomaly, and strong positive vorticity advection, albeit these 
will be at their strongest just to our south and east. At the 
same time, both global model solutions and CAMs suggest an area of
frontogenesis at the 700mb level oriented SW-NE, extending from 
the central mountains and across Denver. This area of 
frontogenesis could be the key for the development of a more 
organized snowband. At the lowest levels, surface winds Saturday 
morning are expected to remain light (<8 mph). However, guidance 
has continued to suggest a broad anticyclonic circulation east of 
Denver, which could help promote some weak upsloping if positioned
at just the right spot. The most obvious limiting factor appears 
to be moisture, with model soundings showing dry air near the 
surface that will need to be overcome for snowfall to reach the 
lower elevations. With all of these ingredients in mind, we have 
concluded that there is about a 30% chance for light snow 
accumulations (up to 0.5 in.) at the lower foothills and east over
parts of the urban corridor. However, snowfall is likely to occur
along a narrow band, meaning that only a small portion of our 
area will end up experiencing the first snowflakes of the season 
at low elevations. We expect this to be a very low-impact event, 
with a high-end scenario (10% chance) snowfall amount of just 
under one inch. Snow activity should exit the area by around noon,
with decreasing cloud cover and temperatures rising into the 
upper 30s and low 40s for the afternoon. 

Sunday morning is shaping up to be quite chilly, particularly for 
our mountains and high valleys, where morning lows will dip into the 
teens and single digits under the mostly clear skies. For the Denver 
metro and plains, morning lows should have no problem dropping into 
the 20s, with perhaps even some upper teens over the usual cold 
spots. We are monitoring the potential for some freezing fog over 
the northeast Colorado plains Sunday morning, although reduced 
visibilities are likely to be the main impact. Afternoon 
temperatures will be similar to Saturday, generally in the 40s, 
due to high clouds filling in from the west ahead of the next 
disturbance. 

Another weakening upper level trough is set to move through the area 
Monday afternoon/evening, bringing another round of 1.0 to 2.0 inch 
snow totals for our mountains. We are fairly confident in 
temperatures staying at seasonal averages or slightly below average 
through at least Wednesday. However, long-range guidance has trended 
slightly drier and warmer late next week and into next weekend, with 
perhaps only a few shortwave passages that would bring sporadic snow 
chances for the mountains, or a few snowflakes at best for the 
Denver metro and plains.

.AVIATION /06Z TAFS through 06Z Sunday/...
Issued at 1020 PM MST Fri Nov 20 2026

Radar shows elevated banding developing over the Front Range and
urban corridor, with generally SCT cloud decks ~050-070 forming
across the Denver metro. Expect increasing potential for periods
of BKN CIGS after sunrise, with greater confidence for the late
morning period when the potential for flurries and light SHSN will
be highest (~30%) for the terminals. There should be a steady
lifting of CIGS and bases through the afternoon from north to
south. Near sunrise Sun, low stratus could encroach on KDEN from
the east. Confidence is low in any direct impacts to the terminal
at this time, but there's sufficient a signal to incorporate VCFG
starting ~13Z. The window for impacts, if any, looks to be 
relatively short (~13-16Z).

As for winds, anticipating current SSE/SE flow to mostly hold,
although some variability in direction is likely throughout the
day, with period of more easterly or southerly flow not out of the
question for KDEN/KAPA. Regardless, prevailing wind speeds are 
forecast to remain below 10 kts.
