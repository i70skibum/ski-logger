.DISCUSSION /Through Thursday/...
Issued at 1214 AM MST Fri Nov 20 2026

Our next approaching storm system is visible on satellite imagery 
moving across the Great Basin tonight. Current METAR observations 
show snow showers have begun to move into the northern mountains, 
where southwesterly flow will favor the Park Range. 3-6 inches 
are forecast between today and Saturday, with locally higher 
amounts possible at the highest elevations. As this shortwave approaches,
southwesterly flow will steadily increase as the night progresses
and an 80-90kt 500mb jet moves over Colorado. While there will be
enough moisture for light mountain snow showers today, the main 
weather impacts will be with gusty winds and elevated to near- 
critical fire weather conditions developing across portions of the
plains and Palmer Divide this afternoon. Gusty southwesterly 
winds mixed with above-normal temperatures are expected to drop 
relative humidity (RH) values down to 17%-22% across the plains, 
where 30-40mph winds are expected to develop by late morning. 
There isn't expected to be enough of an overlap with the lowest RH
and gusty winds to warrant any fire weather highlights, but 
elevated fire weather conditions can be expected across much of 
the lower elevations this afternoon. Additionally, with the jet 
overhead, expect strong winds gusting up to 65-70mph at times over
our high mountain peaks, but gusts should generally be 40-50mph 
over the mountains, with some 30-40mph gusts making their way down
the lee of the Front Range. A cold front is expected to slide 
across the forecast area later this afternoon and evening that 
will shift surface winds to the northwest, advecting a much colder
airmass into Colorado. 

With colder temperatures expected behind the front, snow levels will 
drop overnight, and we may see some snow flurries across the lower 
elevations by Saturday morning when temperatures will drop below 
freezing, and QG forcing is greatest as the shortwave passes just to 
our south. Unfortunately, this once robust looking system just 
won't have enough juice to bring any accumulating snowfall to our 
lower elevations, but we may see a few tenths in our high mountain
valleys, southern foothills, and along the Palmer Divide when all
is said and done. Saturday's afternoon high temperatures will be 
roughly 5 to 10 degrees below seasonal values across the forecast 
area, with 30s and 40s forecasted for the plains, and 30s for the 
mountain valleys. 

Brief upper-level ridging is expected to return by Saturday night 
that will bring drier conditions back to Colorado, but won't have 
much impact on the cooler temperatures. Afternoon high temps will 
finally start to resemble November normals, with the plains actually 
looking to be a few degrees below seasonal values through at least 
the middle of next week. Uncertainties remain with the strength and 
track of our next potential shortwave (Monday?), but it's not
looking like any significant precipitation will be possible
outside of a few waves of light mountain snowfall through the 
forecast period.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Saturday/...
Issued at 1207 PM MST Fri Nov 20 2026

Breezy sustained SW winds of 15-20 kts with gusts to around 30 kts
will prevail at KDEN and KAPA for much of the afternoon. While
gusts at times could exceed 30 kts, it is unlikely they exceed 35
kts. There is more uncertainty in the wind forecast for KBJC as 
they tend to be sheltered from these SW winds. Recent obs indicate
a more northerly direction, but it is still about a 60% chance
winds shift to the SW rather than a 40% chance to remain N or NW.  
A more westerly flow will occur late this afternoon into the 
evening along with winds speeds weakening after sunset. Tonight 
KDEN and KAPA will favor a SE flow before turning SSW for 
Saturday afternoon. Expect KBJC to shift to NW/W drainage flow and
SE flow to develop tomorrow.

Cloud cover should also increase tonight with BKN layers 
developing near 030-050 and overcast conditions above. Cloud are
likely to lift higher as the day continues Saturday. There is 
~20% chance a snow shower could develop near KBJC or KAPA, but no
visibility reductions are expected.
