.DISCUSSION /Through Sunday/...
Issued at 1120 PM MST Sun Nov 22 2026

While minimal weather impacts are expected across the forecast area 
this holiday week, a few disturbances will move through the 
Intermountain West, bringing periodic chances for light snowfall 
across the mountains. Overall, relatively quiet weather is expected 
through the forecast period, with a gradual warming trend beginning 
by mid-week. 

The first disturbance is still on track to move across the region
later today, potentially creating some travel headaches along the
high mountain passes and I-70 mountain corridor this afternoon 
and evening. But before it arrives, portions of Lincoln, Elbert, 
and Weld Counties will have to contend with some patchy freezing 
fog this morning, with current observations showing low stratus 
are already beginning to settle in. While a Dense Fog Advisory
doesn't seem warranted at this time, conditions will be monitored
to see how things evolve, but folks should be prepared for some
patchy fog out there this morning, with the greatest impacts
expected along I-70 through Elbert and Lincoln Counties. 

With regards to today's storm system, while the greatest QG 
forcing will remain to our south, a brief period of enhanced QG 
ascent is expected as the shortwave tracks across the Rockies. 
Forecast soundings show a deeply saturated profile, with the DGZ 
situated within the layer of strongest lift. Additionally, lapse 
rates approaching 9C/km are expected for a few hours leading up to
sunset, which could support the development of a few convective 
snow showers. Given the steep lapse rates and favorable DGZ 
placement, snowfall rates could briefly exceed what the broader- 
scale forcing might suggest. Thus, while overall snowfall 
accumulations are expected to remain relatively light, briefly 
heavier convective snow showers could create locally hazardous 
travel conditions, especially across mountain passes and the I-70
mountain corridor.

QPF has been trended upward to account for this potential and better 
align with recent guidance. Generally, expecting between 1-3 
inches of snowfall along the I-70 mountain corridor and 
Continental Divide, although some locally higher amounts cannot be
ruled out where all the ingredients come together. The remainder 
of the mountains should see around an inch or less, with a few 
tenths to a half inch expected for elevations as low as 7,500 
feet. PoPs and QPF have also been extended to include the Palmer 
Divide and adjacent plains, where a few tenths to a tenth of an 
inch of snowfall are now included in the latest forecast package. 
Temperatures will remain below seasonal averages today and 
Tuesday. 

Subsidence will develop Tuesday morning behind the departing system 
as an amplified upper-level ridge shifts eastward across the 
Rockies, bringing an end to snow showers and a return to warmer and 
drier conditions across the forecast area. Afternoon high 
temperatures will gradually climb through the week, with Thursday's 
holiday weather shaping up to be mild and dry. Highs are expected to 
reach the mid 50s along the urban corridor and mid 40s across the 
mountain valleys. 

Looking ahead, ensembles continue to hint at another round of 
mountain snow potentially arriving by Friday morning, while the 
plains are expected to remain dry. Temperatures will trend well 
above seasonal averages heading into the weekend.

to NE flow around 16-17Z.
