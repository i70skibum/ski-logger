.DISCUSSION /Through Monday/...
Issued at 1134 PM MST Mon Nov 16 2026

We're starting to see stratus and fog increase again across
portions of the northeast plains tonight, with some patchy dense
fog noted near the edge of the stratus deck in Weld county.
Guidance again favors fog across the Poudre/South Platte River
valleys east of I-25 late tonight into the mid-morning hours.
There's some signals we could get dense fog again across 
Weld/Morgan/Logan counties but will punt a decision on Dense Fog 
Advisories for a couple hours longer.

Tuesday should be another warm and dry day across the forecast
area, with southwesterly flow aloft increasing through the day
ahead of a sharp shortwave. That shortwave is expected to push
through/north of the region Tuesday night, before quickly lifting
into the Dakotas by Wednesday afternoon.

Guidance produces a well-defined area of rain and snow across Utah
during the day Tuesday - associated with the mid-level 
trough/front - which quickly advances into the western fringes of
our CWA late Tuesday evening. High resolution guidance continues
to be more bullish with the eastward extent of precip/snow with 
this wave, producing a few inches of snow across the Park Range 
and an inch or so for most of the central/northern mountains 
overnight. Steep lapse rates and mid-level frontogenesis, along 
with 0-2km mean winds near 30-35kt would favor some 
convective/snow squall like structures across the northern 
mountains that could produce some briefly moderate/heavy snow 
before moisture quickly dries up overnight. Models also hold on to
a little bit of additional light snow for the Park Range 
Wednesday morning before completely drying out the boundary layer 
after noon.

While the snow is a welcome arrival, this system will also produce
quite a bit of wind for the Front Range. Cross-barrier flow
increases to around 50kt Wednesday morning in a substantially
subsident regime between 800-500mb. Both the RRFS and HRRR
continue to advertise >60kt gusts across northern Front Range
mountains and higher foothills, and I don't see a reason to make
any changes to the existing High Wind Watch at this time. The main
question at this point is if any of these stronger mountain wave
winds make it into the I-25 corridor before the wave breaks down.
There is not a clear signal with tonight's guidance, but there is
good agreement that the flow will weaken Wednesday afternoon
leading to some gusty but not significantly strong winds across
the I-25 corridor and plains. The main concern there will be fire
weather - see the section further below for additional discussion.

Thursday looks to be a quieter day across the region in between
storm systems. Temperatures should moderate a bit across the
higher elevations with highs remaining in the low to mid 50s
across the plains.

The end of week system still looks to take a more favorable track
for snow across the higher elevations. A broad upper low is 
expected to transition back to an open wave as it approaches the 
region, with a couple of shortwaves moving through Colorado as 
they rotate around the base of the trough. This leaves most of the
higher elevations in a broad, moist west-southwesterly flow aloft
for about 24-36 hours from Friday through Saturday before the 
final shortwave lifts out of the region during the day Saturday. 
Of course, a WSW direction is still modestly unfavorable for a lot
of our terrain, but the longer duration of this event would at 
least provide a few inches for most of our mountains. While the 
day shift was (understandably) bearish about any chances for 
precipitation across the plains, it was interesting to see the 
GFS/ECMWF/ICON all throw some light QPF across some of the lower 
elevations early Saturday, with a slightly more favorable look at 
500mb. Given what looks to be a low predictability synoptic setup 
across the west, I suppose this period is worth watching if you're
desperate to see some flakes fly east of the divide.

Beyond that storm system, guidance does indicate a fairly active
storm track across the western CONUS, with chances for light
precipitation (mainly over the mountains) every few days. Nothing 
particularly stands out at this point, but NBM seems to capture 
the chances for mountain snow well enough.

.AVIATION /06Z TAFS through 06Z Wednesday/...
Issued at 1043 PM MST Mon Nov 16 2026

VFR is expected to prevail through the TAF period at the Denver 
airports. Fog will develop again to the north to northeast of 
Denver. The fog will approach DEN again (5-10 miles), but 
southwest winds will keep drier air over DEN. These southwest 
winds will persist through the nightand into Tuesday morning. A

expected to be north to northeast of DEN. If this occurs, 
southwest winds should prevail throughout Tuesday afternoon and 
into Tuesday night. However, a few models show the cyclone very 
close to or over DEN. This could result in northeast winds for a

.FIRE WEATHER...
Issued at 1134 PM MST Mon Nov 16 2026

Near critical fire weather conditions are likely at times from
late Wednesday morning through the afternoon hours behind a trough
passage. Guidance indicates a remarkably dry, subsident airmass in
the wake of a shortwave which passes north of the region early
Wednesday morning... with dew point temperatures falling to the
single digits (or perhaps below 0F in the Foothills). We have
relatively high confidence that humidity values could fall to as
low as 10% across portions of the southern Foothills and Denver
metro during the afternoon hours despite high temperatures only
reaching the mid 50s.

The more difficult aspect of the forecast remains the wind. A
brief mountain wave event is likely to be ongoing Wednesday
morning, but should start to transition to more of a gradient
wind during the daytime as the wave diminishes and the flow aloft
decreases. Evening guidance varies quite a bit on when and what 
that transition looks like. Typical events feature a couple of 
channels of enhanced wind across the metro (typically something 
from BDU- EIK to somewhere near/north of DEN and one near the 
southern C-470 corridor) where brief periods of Red Flag 
conditions would be likely, while most other locations remain in 
light winds. We did contemplate a broad Fire Weather Watch, but 
would like to see more overlap in wind/RH before committing to any
zone based product.

Additional wind event looks plausible with the next trough passage
towards the end of the week, but at this time the wind looks to
generally settle south of I-70... and the event also should
feature more moisture.
