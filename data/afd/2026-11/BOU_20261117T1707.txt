.DISCUSSION /Through Tuesday/...
Issued at 234 PM MST Tue Nov 17 2026

Today has been a pleasant day with high temperatures topping out in 
the upper 50s for the majority of the plains. A trough currently 
located over Nevada will approach Colorado tonight and move through 
on Wednesday. This will bring some snow to the central and northern 
mountains tonight and into Wednesday morning. The Park Range 
continues to look most favored for 2-4 inches of snow. Our central 
and northern mountains are forecast to see a dusting to perhaps an 
inch or two of snow. Modeling has trended to a slight increase in 
moisture leading to the slight bump in snowfall totals. Some briefly 
moderate to heavy snow is possible overnight given steep lapse rates.

Wednesday is shaping up to be an impactful day for the majority of 
our area. Some ingredients appear increasingly likely to come into 
place for a Bora event producing high winds. Subsidence and cross 
barrier, near-westerly flow of 50-60 kts looks likely. Our area will 
be in the right exit region of the jet in the morning before the jet 
is nearly overhead by Wednesday afternoon. Some high-resolution 
model soundings continues to show a stable layer/inversion in the 
600 to 700 mb range, which would help to enhance the already strong 
winds.

Gusty winds look to first begin in the northern mountains as early 
as 11pm tonight. Stronger winds should slowly spread to the east 
into the northern foothills as we progress through Wednesday 
morning. Wind gusts between 60 and 75 mph are expected with perhaps 
a few gusts stronger than that at the highest peaks in the northern 
Front Range mountains and foothills. DESI REFS probabilities 
highlight this potential well with increasing probabilities (50-70% 
chance) of gusts >70 mph with the greatest chances for locations 
above 9,000 feet. We have upgraded to a High Wind Warning in these 
locations from 11pm Tuesday through 11am Wednesday.

As of now, high-resolution model guidance shows the peak of the 
winds along the Hwy 93 corridor- Boulder to Golden- between 7am and 
noon Wednesday. There is increased confidence compared to yesterday 
of a slightly stronger surface pressure gradient and stronger 
momentum transfer. This would introduce a few hour window of wind 
gusts of 40-50 mph with the potential for some isolated locations 
seeing slightly stronger wind gusts in this corridor. These stronger 
winds could possibly extend as far east as KBJC to KLMO, but there 
is uncertainty on how far east the strongest winds could make it. 
Once we reach late Wednesday morning into the afternoon, areas along 
our far northern tier, including the I-25 corridor north of Fort 
Collins, could see gusts of 40-50 mph with some isolated stronger 
wind gusts possible. As is typical in these setups, narrow channels 
of 30 to 45 mph wind gusts could extend to the east across southern 
Weld County and in the southern Denver metro. 18z modeling shows the 
potential for a secondary push of stronger wind gusts along the Hwy 
93 corridor early Wednesday afternoon as cold air advection 
continues, but this scenario remains rather uncertain. In addition, 
we have continued to trend dewpoints lower given the increased 
subsidence for Wednesday morning and afternoon. Elevated to critical 
fire weather conditions are expected. See the Fire Weather 
Discussion for more details.

Thursday will feature a much calmer weather pattern with weak 
ridging aloft. High temperatures will remain in the mid to upper 50s 
for the plains. The next storm system will move into the area on 
Thursday night into Friday. A trough will once again move in, but 
take a slightly more southerly and slower track compared to 
Wednesday's trough. A few shortwaves associated with the trough will 
bring precipitation chances on Friday and Saturday to our area. The 
highest confidence of any snowfall accumulations remains for our 
central and northern mountains given the flow aloft. As we get into 
Friday night and Saturday morning, some light rain showers are 
possible with perhaps a few snowflakes making it into the lower 
elevations. These chances remain rather unimpressive- only about 20-
30% of ensemble guidance shows any sort of light dusting. We do note 
that the last few runs of both the ECMWF and GFS deterministic 
models do try to bring some flakes into the Denver metro on Saturday 
morning as a surface low develops and brings light upslope winds to 
the area. As previously noted, this will be a period to keep an eye 
on.

After Saturday, ensemble guidance is in good agreement that below 
normal temperatures and on and off precipitation chances look to 
continue through much of next week. This should finally bring us our 
first prolonged taste of some cooler temperatures to the lower 
elevations!

.AVIATION /00Z TAFS through 00Z Thursday/...
Issued at 505 PM MST Tue Nov 17 2026

Current observations show the cyclone previously located between 
KAPA and KDEN is weakening and moving off to the east. However, 
gusty southwest winds will continue to be possible over KAPA for 
another hour or two before the more typical SSW drainage winds kick 
in by ~3Z at 10-15KT. Winds at KDEN are mainly northeasterly at this 
time and may shift to the northwest briefly before SW drainage winds 
become established at ~2Z. We generally expect S-SW winds across the 
Denver metro tonight before channels of gusty westerly winds begin 
to form around sunrise (12Z). 

There is considerable uncertainty regarding the exact location of 
these channels or areas of westerly downsloping winds, gusting up to 
30KT. At this time, the timing seems to be most favorable in the 
late morning, between 15Z and 18Z. Given the possibility of mountain 
wave enhancement, periods of easterly winds cannot be ruled out 
if a rotor were to form over one of the airports. We expect winds 
to diminish by 21Z, with a gradual shift to the E-SE for late 
afternoon.

.FIRE WEATHER...
Issued at 240 PM MST Tue Nov 17 2026

Elevated to critical fire weather conditions are 
increasingly likely for much of Wednesday. As a result, we have 
issued a Red Flag Warning for the southern Front Range foothills and 
the Palmer Divide from 8am to 5pm Wednesday. We have the most 
confidence that these locations will see a several hour overlap 
between low relative humidity and wind gusts exceeding 25-30 mph. 
Confidence is not high enough on the exact locations of any narrow 
channels of stronger winds possible across the I-25 corridor (from 
Centennial to Fort Collins and eastward). Note that these winds 
would likely reach thresholds for near-critical fire weather 
conditions. We are confident that relative humidity values will drop 
to below 20% for nearly all of the Red Flag Warning and Fire Weather 
Watch locations. Some locations could see relative humidity drop 
into the upper single digits to low teens. For the northern Front 
Range mountains and foothills, RH values are likely to be slightly 
higher than other locations with gusty winds decreasing by the early 
afternoon. If confidence were to increase in the overlap between 
RH/winds for the northern Front Range mountains and foothills and/or 
the placement of the strongest winds for the adjacent plains, the 
Red Flag Warning could need to be expanded in a future package.

Gusty winds are possible on Friday and Saturday with the next
storm system mainly for the high country and southern portions of
our area. More moisture is expected at this time, so there is only
a low risk for elevated fire weather conditions.
