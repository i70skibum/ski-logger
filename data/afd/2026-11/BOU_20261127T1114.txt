.DISCUSSION /Through Thursday/...
Issued at 1121 PM MST Thu Nov 26 2026

The system for this weekend is still on track. We're starting to 
see winds increase in the higher terrain, with gusts around 30 to 
50mph already. Northwest flow aloft will continue to strengthen 
through Friday afternoon, remaining strong through late Saturday. 
Wind gusts above tree line will be around 50 to 70mph late Friday 
morning through the early afternoon. The strongest winds will be 
confined to the highest elevations, but wind gusts will still
reach around 30 to 50mph in the northern foothills. The strong 
northwest winds are expected to move off of the Cheyenne Ridge and
into our northern counties in the early morning, producing gusts 
around 30 to 50mph in the northern plains. 

While snow could start in the mountains on Friday, the peak lifting 
from the upper level shortwave and left entrance of the upper jet
will hit early Saturday morning continuing into the early 
afternoon. Mountains snow will start to increase overnight 
Friday into Saturday, with the heaviest snow Saturday morning and
early afternoon. There will be two potential big impacts to 
mountain travel on Saturday. The first potential impact is 
blowing snow. Wind gusts will be around 50 to 60mph at times, 
which when combined with fresh snow on the ground and snow falling
around 0.5 to 1 inch an hour at times, could cause serious 
visibility issues. The second potential impact is snow squalls. 
Strong frontogenesis and some weak instability could be enough for
snow squalls to develop in the mountains. Snow squalls cause 
rapid changes to visibilities and driving conditions, creating 
very hazardous travel. QPF amounts continue to trend higher for 
our higher elevations. This combined with the strong winds leading
to blowing snow and the holiday weekend travel has led us to 
upgrade our Park Range zone to a Winter Storm Warning. As of right
now, we've left our Front Range zones in a Winter Weather 
Advisory, due to the lower snow totals, but travel conditions 
could still be very hazardous if snow banding or snow squalls 
develop. 

The timing of the cold front Saturday is still uncertain. A few 
of the high res models are trending faster, with the front 
entering our northern counties between 7 and 8am, reaching the 
metro around 9, and exiting our southern counties by 11am (which 
is before the GFS even brings it into our northern counties). If 
this happens, it would give the plains a much quicker transition 
from rain to snow and colder afternoon temperatures. We are 
leaning towards the quicker front solutions and have adjusted the 
forecast accordingly. A trace to a few tenths of snow still looks
possible for the metro Saturday afternoon, but it will depend on
that front timing. the south metro, Palmer, and southern 
foothills will be favored for better precip chances and snow 
totals given the north/northeast flow behind the front. We are
also monitoring the potential for snow bands to develop due to 
the strong frontogenesis. Areas that see a heavier snow bands 
will be more likely for brief snow accumulations on roads and 
potential travel impacts. 

Snow chances will decrease Saturday evening as the shortwave and
jet move out of our area. Dry conditions will prevail Sunday 
through Tuesday for much of the area, with warmer temperature for 
the start of next week. We could see a weak upper level 
disturbance bring brief snow chances to the mountains on Monday, 
but little to no impacts are expected. Models are showing an upper
level low developing off the southwest coast on Tuesday, moving 
east over the four corners and then near our area. The exact 
progression of the low is still uncertain in both timing and 
location, but this will be our next chance for both mountain and 
plains snowfall. Right now, the best chance for precip with this 
system will be late Wednesday into Thursday. Temperatures are 
forecast to drop back closer to normal behind the cold front 
associated with this system.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Saturday/...
Issued at 1112 AM MST Fri Nov 27 2026

VFR conditions expected to prevail through 18Z tomorrow, with 
ceilings lowering after a frontal passage. Winds have been fairly 
gusty at KDEN and KBJC, with 30-40kt gusts at KBJC and 20-30kt gusts 
at KDEN recorded in the last couple hours. TDEN has also picked up 
on a weak boundary east of KDEN, with northeasterly winds on the 
other side of it. While it seems to have stalled, should that 
boundary move more to the west, KDEN could see a period of light 
easterly winds. That being said, the stronger westerly winds are 
expected to continue into the afternoon and weaken around 23-24Z. By 
04-05Z, winds at all three TAF sites should settle into a light 
southerly flow and stay there overnight, with a brief period of 
light and variable in the early morning.  

Another period of gusty (20-30kt) westerly winds is expected around 
16-17Z tomorrow for KDEN and KBJC ahead of a cold front. There's 
some uncertainty in the exact timing of the frontal passage; while 
our current forecasts favors an earlier passage, around 18Z, recent 
model runs have tended towards a slightly later passage, around 19-
20Z. Regardless of the timing, confidence is high in there being 
even stronger northerly winds, with gusts of 35-40kts, behind the 
front. This system will also bring a chance for precipitation to the 
Denver metro area, with any showers starting as rain and 
transitioning to snow as the front moves through. Snow showers 
should be mostly light, but we can't rule out the possibility for 
heavier snowfall rates and/or a snow squall to briefly drop 
visibilities. As such, we've included a TEMPO group at the end of
KDEN's TAF for reduced visibilities and snow showers.
