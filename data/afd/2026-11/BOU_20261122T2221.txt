.DISCUSSION /Through Sunday/...
Issued at 101 PM MST Sun Nov 22 2026

Current satellite imagery shows high clouds trekking across the NE
plains of Colorado. The upper air pattern features slight ridging
aloft as we wait for the shortwave to move in off the coast of 
California. Temperatures this afternoon across the South Platte 
River valley may only reach the lower 40s compared to mid/upper 
40s in the Denver metro as a result of this mornings fog and low 
clouds. A Denver cyclone will develop today and coupled with 
southerly downsloping winds off the Palmer Divide, generate breezy
conditions for Elbert, Lincoln, and areas east of DIA. Low 
temperatures for the next few nights will reach the 20s with 
valleys and naturally colder spots approaching the upper teens.
Freezing fog is also possible Monday morning for Lincoln and
eastern Elbert counties. Reduced visiblities down to 0.25-0.50 sm
are possible. 

Ensemble and deterministic models are in good agreement for a 
shortwave to move across the Central Rockies tomorrow. This 
system should bring enough lift and moisture to the mountains to 
produce our next chance of snow for the higher terrain (40-60%). 
Snowfall is likely to accumulate in the Mosquito Range, Gore 
Range, with lighter accumulations across the Continental Divide 
and Front Range. Snow will start Monday afternoon before tapering
off early Tuesday morning. The greatest accumulation's appear to 
be focused on the Gore and Mosquito ranges, where the probability 
of 24 hr snowfall >1" is 60-70% and >2" is ~40-50%. Portions of 
the Palmer Divide and Lincoln county still have a ~10-20% chance 
to see measurable snowfall. Due to the expected cloud cover 
tomorrow, forecasted high temperatures should remain in the lower 
40s for most of the area. Tuesday's sky will clear with cool high 
temperatures once again in the upper 40s. 

Upper-level ridging creeps its way back into Colorado Wednesday as 
the axis should be centered across our area by the afternoon. 
Ensemble guidance is in good agreement this will induce gradual 
warming through the weekend. Thanksgiving day looks to be mild in 
the 50s and dry with no impactful weather. Ensembles for the weekend 
depict a large high pressure to develop over the western coast causing
NW flow aloft for the weekend. This NW direction will lead to
upslope flow for the northern and central mountains which may
produce periods of snow showers. Ensembles are in disagreement 
regarding the location of jets and other factors that could 
influence snowfall amounts. For now this will be something to 
just keep an eye on. However, our lower elevations are looking 
likely to stay dry through this weekend.
