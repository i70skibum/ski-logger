.KEY MESSAGES... 

- Hazardous travel tonight - Saturday evening for the northern
  mountains and I-70 mountain corridor due to snow and blowing
  snow. Snowfall amounts greater than 12" likely in the northern 
  tier of mountains.

- Snow squalls possible with greatest threat in the mountains late
  morning to early afternoon Saturday.

  I-25 Corridor Saturday to early Saturday evening. Brief heavy

  season.

  this evening, spreading across all the plains Saturday.

- Return of mostly dry weather Sunday - Tuesday, next disturbance
  may arrive by Wednesday or Thursday.

.DISCUSSION /Through Friday/...
Issued at 155 PM MST Fri Nov 27 2026

Gusty winds but mild temperatures are present along the Front
Range this afternoon. The winds will slowly decrease in the lower
elevations with the loss of daytime heating and mixing, but remain
quite gusty over the higher foothills and mountains where gusts of
40-60 mph will continue through the night under the influence of
strong westerly flow aloft. We are starting to see the first signs
of our next snow event, with mid level clouds thickening in the
northern mountains and even a few flurries noted on the Rocky
Mountain National Park cam. While moisture is rather limited 
below about 700 mb, we do expect a further but moderated increase
in snow overnight. That will occur as we get into the left exit 
region of the upper level jet and a fast moving shortwave (still 
out in the Pacific) starts to bring large scale lift to the 
forecast area.

That shortwave and associated cold front will sweep across the
area Saturday late morning to early afternoon. A period of pre-
frontal gusty downslope winds to 40-60 mph can be expected across
the Front Range Foothills and adjacent plains, but then expect a
sharp turn to strong, gusty north winds behind the cold front.
There is still uncertainty with the timing of this front.
Yesterday, models were aggressive in speeding this front up with a
morning passage. However, today they've been delaying the frontal
arrival til around noon or shortly thereafter. We still think it
might be late morning as the trough position and precipitation
loading/sub-cloud evaporation in Wyoming would support an earlier
arrival. Whatever the case, strong, gusty winds to 40-55 mph can
be expected on the plains with and just behind the frontal 
passage. Slight anticyclonic turning is also noted, which will
negate the downslope component and combine with synoptic scale 
lift of the trough passage and a brief period of strong 
frontogenesis to allow showers to spread southward across the 
plains. Initially, temperatures may support a little rain, but 
the magnitude of cold advection and surface temperatures dropping
into the 30s would support mostly snow within an hour or so of 
the frontal passage.

The strong frontogenesis, slight instability (even a bit of 
SBCAPE noted along the front), moisture convergence, and neutral
to slightly unstable mid level lapse rates will also bring a 
threat of snow squalls. The highest probability (60%) for those 
would be in the mountains during and just behind the frontal 
passage, and possibly (20-40% chance) down into the foothills and
Palmer Divide where temperatures would be colder and more
conducive to road impacts. The greatest risk time would be with 
the front, starting around 10 am (northern mountains), pushing 
south through early afternoon and ending in the southern portions 
of the forecast area closer to 2 pm. 

Regarding snowfall amounts, many favorable ingredients are coming
together for moderate to heavy snow totals in the high country.
These include synoptic scale lift, strong orographics turning more
northwesterly and eventually favoring Summit County later in the
event, and neutral to slightly unstable lapse rates. While there 
is still some uncertainty, we have modest confidence of 5-12 inch 
totals for the Front Range mountains and Summit County (heaviest 
north of I-70) and 8-16 inch amounts for the northern Gore and 
Park Ranges. If anything, those could be on the light side if we
happen to have more moisture and overproduce tonight. We are
somewhat leery of the heaviest totals offered up by some of the
high resolution models, but something to keep an eye on as we
start this event tonight. On the plains, any accumulation will be
heavily dependent on the intensity and persistence of snow 
showers/bands. At this point, it still looks like areas north of 
metro Denver will only see a brief dusting or no accumulation, but
a growing chance of light accumulations as one heads southward 
through Denver metro and especially into the Palmer Divide and 
southern Front Range foothills due to the deep northerly or even 
slight NNE flow. That would help longevity there. Thus, we've 
bumped up totals a little into the foothills west and southwest of
Denver with 1-3 inches most likely there. We're still carrying a 
50-60% chance of Denver's first official measurable snow (DIA), 
but it would likely be light (less than 0.5") if we do see any 
accumulation.

We still expect snow to taper off fairly quickly from north to
south late Saturday afternoon and early Saturday evening as the 
system quickly ejects to the east. However, some travel impacts 
may linger in the mountains and southern foothills into early 
Sunday morning due to residual road snow. Those conditions will 
improve rather quickly however with return of sunshine, so Sunday 
is shaping up to be a much better post-holiday travel day than 
Saturday. 

Mainly dry weather will then persist into Monday and Tuesday,
although the flow appears to become zonal enough to have a chance
of light snow showers for the high country. After a chilly Sunday,
temperatures will be warming back to above normal levels both
days. 

The latest runs were still trending toward at least a weak closed
low tracking a bit further northward into Colorado toward Thursday.
This will mean another chance of rain/snow for the forecast area 
with temperatures cooling to near normal levels again.

.AVIATION /06Z TAFS through 06Z Sunday/...
Issued at 1042 PM MST Fri Nov 27 2026

VFR conditions expected tonight through early tomorrow morning.
Winds are currently gusting from the northwest at DEN and BJC as
channeled winds have continued tonight. However, expect drainage
flow to return in a few hours at DEN. BJC will likely be gusting
from the west/northwest all night. 

Saturday is still gearing up to be an impactful day due to a
system bringing strong winds and precipitation to all terminals. 
By 15Z-17Z, lee cyclogenesis is favored to develop, allowing gusty
westerly winds up to 20-28 kts for a few hours. A strong cold 
front will then race across the region by 20Z-22Z, which will 
transition winds to the north. Model guidance has been very 
consistent with gusts of 40-50 kts possible with the frontal 
passage. I have upped the wind gusts in the TAF to reflect that 
potential. After the quick burst of strong winds, northerly winds 
should weaken a bit, but expect northerly wind gusts of 20-30 kts 
through 03Z. Models are in good agreement of winds weakening
significantly by 05Z-07Z, with generally light (<10 kts) and
variable winds overnight. 

In terms of precipitation, all terminals have a chance to see
rain/snow showers with the frontal passage. The cold front will
drastically drop temperatures and any precipitation that does fall
during that time should transition from rain to snow. Snow
accumulations should be light (<0.5"), however convective snow
showers will likely reduce visibilities down to 1 - 1 1/2 SM at
times. Ceilings will lower with any precipitation, with guidance
favoring 500-1000 feet. A few low clouds may linger after 23Z-24Z,
but generally expect VFR conditions to a little after that.
