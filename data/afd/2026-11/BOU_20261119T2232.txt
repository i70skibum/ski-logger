.DISCUSSION /Through Thursday/...
Issued at 1241 PM MST Thu Nov 19 2026

Tomorrow, an 80kt 500mb jet will move over the area ahead of the 
approaching shortwave. At 700mb, models have winds around 35kts 
to 55kts. Conditions do not look favorable for mountain wave 
amplification in our area due to the flow being more southwesterly and
a few other factors. However, we are expecting strong winds to be
forced/mixed down to the surface in the late morning through the 
afternoon. Wind gusts across the plains will be around 30 to 50mph
in the afternoon. The one exception will be the northern I-25 
corridor and portions of the northern plains, which tend to remain
more sheltered from the strong winds in southwest flow. In the 
higher elevations, wind gusts will near 50 to 65mph in the late 
morning. A front will approach our northern counties late Friday 
afternoon, turning winds more towards the west/northwest in our 
northern counties. This will allow for stronger winds to finally 
make their way into the northern I-25 corridor and northern 
plains, with gusts around 25mph to 40mph. There will be elevated 
fire weather conditions Friday afternoon due to the winds, but 
relative humidities are forecast to remain above critical 
thresholds. The front will move through the area overnight, 
bringing cooler temperatures for Saturday.

The approaching shortwave, weak orographics, and weak surface 
convergence could be enough to trigger a few showers in the 
higher terrain and plains overnight Friday into the late morning
Saturday. Moisture will be rather limited, so snow accumulations 
will remain on the low end. With the exception of the Park Range, 
which should see slightly higher amounts, snow accumulations will 
generally be a few hundredths to about 3 inches in the higher 
terrain. Chances for at least a trace of snow in the metro remains
on the low end (~20% chance or less). Temperatures will likely 
support at least a rain/snow mix if not all snow Saturday morning 
across the plains, it's just the lack of moisture and lack of a 
strong forcing that's inhibiting snow chances across the plains.

Cooler temperatures will continue across the forecast area 
through this forecast. Low temperatures Sunday through the middle
of next week will be in the teens to 20s. Our next system will 
approach Colorado on Monday, with a shortwave moving through late 
Monday into Tuesday. The timing and positioning of the shortwave 
varies between models, lowering our confidence in precip chances 
with this system. Right now, we expect light snow showers in the 
mountains with a medium chance (30% to 50%) that snow showers make
it onto the plains. If we do end up with some snow over the 
plains, accumulations will be light and impacts are not 
anticipated. The active pattern is expected to continue into later 
next week, but models continue to swing back and forth with the 
potential impacts and confidence in any one model solution is very
low at this time.

.AVIATION /06Z TAFS through 06Z Saturday/...
Issued at 1026 PM MST Thu Nov 19 2026

VFR conditions expected to prevail for all terminals through at
least 06Z Sat. Southerly drainage winds will steadily increase at
KDEN/KAPA through the early morning hours, with a more defined
shift to SW (220-240) winds beginning near 17Z as daytime mixing
develops. Anticipating sustained winds of 15-20 kts will be
prevalent at both terminals through much of the afternoon, with
potential for stronger gusts to around 30 kts, or slightly higher
at KAPA. Much more uncertain wind speed forecast for KBJC, which
tends to be more sheltered from said wind direction. Opted to
lower speeds for the latter, though periods of stronger gusts can
still be expected. Ultimately, the stronger SW flow should just be
more intermittent relative to the other two terminals. Expect a
more westerly shift late afternoon into the evening with a fairly
quick weakening in speeds after sunset.

Favoring development of SCT cloud bases near 040-060 after ~06Z
Sat. There's some potential for occasional CIGS thereafter as well
or even a brief and very light -SHSN, but confidence remains far
too low for any explicit inclusion in the TAFs.
