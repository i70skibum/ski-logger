.DISCUSSION /Through Wednesday/...
Issued at 1115 PM MST Wed Nov 18 2026

The flow aloft will increase on Friday ahead of another trough
axis. This also looks to be the last warm day before a cooler
pattern takes over for the weekend into next week, with highs
reaching the upper 50s to low 60s. 700mb flow reaches about 40kt 
by midday Friday in a broadly subsident regime, meaning that 
widespread wind gusts are expected to develop across the Front 
Range and plains during the afternoon. For the lower elevations, 
elevated fire weather conditions will again be a concern in this 
pattern. Two limiting factors are (1) the northern I-25 corridor 
often ends up at least partially sheltered in SW flow, and (2) 
relative humidity looks to be at least a little bit higher than 
Wednesday.

Across the mountains, moisture will begin to increase during the
morning hours, leading to a gradual increase in snow. With a 
~120kt jet streak remaining nearly stationary over northwestern 
Colorado, one dominant band of light (to at times moderate) snow 
somewhere over northern Colorado, with weaker orographic snow 
showers across the rest of the mountains. Overall moisture is 
still somewhat limited, and there may be a break in between 
shortwaves Friday afternoon into Friday night.

A second shortwave is expected to track through the state on
Saturday. There's enough moisture for another organized round of
light snow, this time focused along and south of the I-70
mountains. Snowfall totals in this period will again be on the
light side. Guidance continues to struggle with the evolution of
this shortwave as is tracks into southwestern Kansas during the
day. Several deterministic models (e.g., ECMWF, RGEM) are 
insistent that a band of snow will spread into the Denver metro 
and/or Palmer Divide during the morning hours Saturday, with 
generally light (roughly a half inch to inch) snowfall amounts 
possible. On the other hand, the GFS and most of the American
short term/regional guidance is weaker with the developing surface
low over the OK panhandle and struggle to produce any QPF outside
of the higher terrain. I have a slight preference towards the
ECMWF solution in this time period and our grids are close to
the previous forecast - which is higher than the current NBM.

This system will also usher in a period of cooler weather across 
the forecast area. Even between systems Sunday and Monday, 
temperatures across the plains will likely settle in the mid to 
upper 40s. Guidance is in good agreement that near to below normal
temperatures will continue through the rest of the forecast 
period with highs sticking in the 40s.

A fairly active pattern also looks likely to continue next week,
with trough working through the region every few days. This means
there's chance for additional mountain snow Monday into Tuesday,
and again sometime late week next week. What system brings the
best chance at snow to the region? It's difficult to say.
Deterministic solutions continue to swing wildly from run to run
in this period. As we've seen with this upcoming Friday/Saturday
system, this pattern continues to have low predictability, and
large day-to-day/run-to-run swings are likely to continue through
most of the upcoming forecast period.
