.DISCUSSION /Through Tuesday/...
Issued at 1134 PM MST Tue Nov 17 2026

The first part of this storm system arrived earlier this evening
as a band of rain and snow progressed from southwest to northeast
across the mountains. There was enough moisture to maintain this
band of precipitation as far east as the continental divide, with
webcams from CDOT and various ski areas showing some brief light
to moderate snow... though accumulations were generally around an
inch. A few orographic snow showers are expected to continue
overnight with minimal additional snowfall totals.

The second part of this system will be the wind. Surface
observations across the higher Front Range foothills have been
slowly increasing over the last couple of hours, with a few gusts
in the 45-50 mph range noted in the last hour or so. Near term
guidance has been too bullish with the initial development of the
mountain wave, and I would expect that we continue to see a
slow increase in wind gusts overnight before the mountain wave
begins to become better established closer to 12z. From an
ingredients perspective just about everything is "marginal". While
the ridgetop/cross-barrier flow is around 45-55kt, the stable
layer is somewhat diffuse and a bit higher than you'd expect for a
more classic mountain wave. The setup is also rather brief - with
the wave quickly diminishing after 18z Wednesday when winds 
transition to more of a subsident/gradient type regime. Ultimately
the current headlines seem representative enough. We still expect
a few gusts around 75 mph over the foothills with a few gusts to 
around 60 mph possible for the typical wind prone areas near the 
base of the foothills. These winds will also produce some fire 
weather concerns during the day (discussed further below).

Thursday still looks to be a quiet day across the forecast area as
we sit in between systems in a dry southwesterly flow aloft.
Forecast high temperatures will be back in the 50s again across
most of the plains.

Our next system of interest is still on track to arrive on Friday.
Across the plains, Friday looks mild and breezy in a broad
southwesterly flow regime. Meanwhile, boundary layer moisture
should increase across the higher elevations, and should allow 
mountain snow showers to develop as early as Friday morning. The
first of two shortwaves races across the northern half of the
state during the day, and aided by a 110kt jet streak... this
could produce a band or two of light to moderate snow
accumulations across the central/northern mountains.

The second of two shortwaves associated with the large scale
trough then pivots through the region Friday night into Saturday.
This should provide another round of snow across most of the
higher elevations. There's been noteworthy shifts with the 
timing/orientation of this wave in relation to the northern stream
vort max in this period, and it seems likely there are still 
several shifts to go here. In the meantime, this interim solution 
is a bit wetter across the plains, with a handful of deterministic
solutions (and about 30-50% of ensemble members) producing some 
very light snow accumulation across portions of northern Colorado.
I would like to see a few more cycles of both physics-based and 
AI ensemble guidance before getting to enthused about precip 
chances along the I-25 corridor.

Guidance throws another weak shortwave towards our forecast area
sometime early next week, with generally light QPF amounts over
the mountains. There's also a good signal that cooler weather will
arrive and stick around through at least the first half of next
week, with temperatures near/below normal favored.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Thursday/...
Issued at 1030 AM MST Wed Nov 18 2026

There remains relatively high uncertainty on the exact locations 
of the narrow channels of stronger westerly/northwesterly winds
which could affect KAPA and KDEN over the next few hours. If 
these channels are not located directly over the site, we expect 
weak easterly winds. At this hour, one of these channels is 
located over KAPA with some gusts at or slightly above 30 kts. We 
expect winds to weaken at KAPA by about 20z. At KDEN, the 
potential remains over the next few hours for gusty winds given 
several observations 5-10 miles west of KDEN nearing 25-30 kts. We
remain most confident that KBJC will see wind gusts around 30 kts
for the next few hours due to its position closer to the higher 
terrain before weakening this afternoon.

Winds are forecast to turn clockwise and start northeast this 
afternoon. They will eventually turn to drainage and/or a modified
drainage this evening. Winds could approach 12-15 kts at times, 
but are forecast to be much weaker than this morning and early 
afternoon. For Thursday afternoon, after a brief period of vrb 
winds in the morning, winds are forecast to turn to the northeast, 
but remain under 10 kts.

.FIRE WEATHER...
Issued at 1134 PM MST Tue Nov 17 2026

Most of the day Wednesday will feature elevated to critical fire
weather conditions across the lower foothills and adjacent plains.
While we have fairly high confidence in both the morning wind and
the afternoon's very dry humidity, this still remains a very 
challenging fire weather forecast.

The lingering question tonight is how long gusty winds persist
into the early afternoon hours. Guidance quickly weakens the winds
aloft beyond 18z, with the pressure gradient also relaxing in this
timeframe. High resolution guidance continues to slowly back off
on the afternoon wind gust potential outside of the foothills as
well. There will likely be some sporadic gusts across the base of
the foothills, where we have slightly higher confidence in the
overlap of critical RH and wind gusts. To the east, a few channels
of enhanced wind still appear likely, but the consensus from the
evening shift forecasters was that we will not see sufficient
spatial/temporal coverage across 240/243 to justify a Red Flag
Warning.
