.DISCUSSION /Through Wednesday/...
Issued at 110 PM MST Wed Nov 25 2026

Flat upper level ridging will keep dry conditions in place through
Friday, along with a warming trend. For tonight, mid and high
clouds will pass overhead but some late night clearing and a dry
airmass should still support near normal low temperatures.
Sunshine most of Thanksgiving Day and further upper level
ridging, warming aloft, and weakening inversions will aid high
temperatures warming several degrees above normal. We think most
of the plains will be able to reach the 50s, coolest (near 50F)  
in the far northeast and warmest (approaching 60F) in Denver. 
Winds will remain light. 

On Friday, the flow aloft will strengthen as an upper level low
and associated shortwave move across southern Canada and the
Northern Rockies, while an upper high holds over the east Central
Pacific. That means increasing winds for our mountains and
foothills, with gusts to 35-50 mph expected in wind prone areas.
The northern plains near the Wyoming and Nebraska borders will 
also see winds increase with gusts to 30-35 mph. Fire weather
conditions will be elevated due to the winds, but humidity levels
are still forecast well above critical thresholds. Mid and upper
level moisture will likely bring more clouds, and perhaps a couple
light late day snow showers for the northern mountains. Snow
showers will gradually increase in the mountains Friday night as
orographics and moisture levels improve, with the heaviest snow 
and more significant post-Thanksgiving travel impacts expected by
Saturday. 

That heavier mountain snow (and blowing snow) will be driven by an
upper level disturbance racing off the Pacific and across the
Northern/Central Rockies on Saturday. We'll be getting into the
left exit region of a 120 kt jet, with improving QG forcing,
stronger orographics, and neutral lapse rates. Therefore, all the
ingredients will be in place for at least a moderate snowfall
event. The only ingredient that would be more uncertain at this
point would be the moisture. Model and ensemble trends have 
generally been upward with QPF given the GFS and GEFS members 
were also coming more in line with the stronger/slightly farther 
south solution. Several inches of snow are now likely for the high
country, with potential high end of over a foot in the northern 
Gore and Park Ranges. We'll likely need an Advisory for the 
mountains Friday night - Saturday night for the mountains, with 
possible Watch/Warning for the northern Gore/Park Ranges in 
upcoming forecast packages if the trends remain favorable.
Saturday will not be the day for your post-Thanksgiving travel if
heading through the Colorado mountains. Plan on Friday or Sunday
(only lingering morning slick spots) for a safer journey. 

For the plains and I-25 Urban Corridor, the depth and intensity 
of the trough offers up additional uncertainty in the forecast.
Given the favorable ingredients mentioned above, it would not 
surprise us to see more widespread/numerous showers spread off 
the higher terrain. The highest odds of this happening would be 
behind the strong frontal surge on Saturday. If this can link up 
with the forcing of the upper level disturbance, then higher 
coverage and potential for banded snow would be possible. While 
most areas would likely see limited accumulations due to marginal 
temperatures, it should be noted that localized heavier bands of 
snow up to 2-4" would be possible (20-30% chance) in this 
scenario. We'll continue to monitor the forcing mechanisms and 
timing closely to see if they can link up.

Beyond that, dry weather is expected to redevelop by late Saturday
night and hold through Sunday night. The next chance of snow 
would then start in the mountains by Monday or Monday night as a 
weakening disturbance pushes through the Central Rockies. The 
relatively active weather pattern (at least for the mountains) 
will likely remain in place with additional disturbances expected 
by mid week and another late next week. After a colder weekend, 
temperatures are expected to warm back a few degrees above normal
next week.
