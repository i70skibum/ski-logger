.DISCUSSION /Through Tuesday/...
Issued at 1021 PM MST Tue Nov 24 2026

Above-normal temperatures and dry conditions will prevail through 
much of the work week. Weak upper level ridging will be near the 
area as a strong upper low sits to our east and a trough moves onto 
the West Coast Wednesday. For Thanksgiving, temperatures will be in 
the upper 40s to mid 50s for the plains and the 30s and 40s for the 
higher terrain. Winds will be on the lighter side for the plains 
(generally less than 15mph) with mostly sunny skies on Thursday. 
Temperatures will peak on Friday with highs in the low 60s for much 
of the plains. 

On Friday, an upper level low is forecast to develop to our 
north/northeast over Canada and a strong high is forecast to develop 
to our southwest over the Pacific. This will put us in an area of 
strong northwest winds aloft. Winds will start to pick up in the 
higher terrain overnight Thursday into Friday, peaking Saturday 
morning. Stronger winds could make it onto the plains on Saturday
as a cold front is dragged through the area. However, there is 
still a lot of model uncertainty with the system this weekend. The
ECMWF is still showing a more potent shortwave moving through on 
Saturday with a strong cold front. While the GFS has a much more 
subtle shortwave and weaker front. Other models are more on board 
with the Euro than the GFS. Also, the GFS is trending slightly 
stronger with the front in latest runs. This has us leaning more 
towards the stronger front and better precip chances solution fort
his weekend. Confidence is high in snow falling in our northern 
mountains, with decreasing confidence as we go further south and 
further east on the plains. Chances for snow on the plains will 
depend on the shortwave and timing/strength of the front, with 
the current NBM at a low to medium chance (10% to 40%) for at 
least a trace of snow across the plains by Sunday morning. 

Temperatures will drop closer to normal for the weekend, with highs 
in the 40s and low 50s. Forecast uncertainty continues for early 
next week as models show very different solutions on the potential 
progression of another shortwave and front Monday.
