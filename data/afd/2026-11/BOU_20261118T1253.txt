.DISCUSSION /Through Wednesday/...
Issued at 1239 PM MST Wed Nov 18 2026

Looking at Water Vapor, the upper level shortwave is moving
through the area to the east, taking the strongest upper level 
winds with it. Winds will gradually decrease into the evening, 
dropping off significantly a little after sunset. We'll see very 
brief upper level ridging in between our two systems on Thursday. 
An upper level trough will move onto the northwest coast of the
U.S. on Thursday, approaching the area on Friday. A little 
shortwave will precede the main trough late Thursday into early 
Friday, bringing snow chances to the mountains during this time. 
Pacific moisture will move into the higher terrain with this 
system at low to mid levels, helping with precip chances in the 
mountains. Moisture will generally remain higher to the west of 
our area, with the best chances of snow for our CWA in the Park 
Range.

The main trough will approach the region late Friday, moving through 
on Saturday. Surface low pressure will develop on the lee side of 
the mountains ahead of this system. This low will drag a cold front 
through the area late Friday into early Saturday, bringing much 
cooler temperatures.

We could briefly sit in the right entrance of the upper level jet 
Saturday morning. At the same time we'll see broad upper level 
support ahead of the trough. Unfortunately, moisture throughout 
the column doesn't look great, but there should be enough for snow
showers in the higher terrain and potentially some light showers 
in the plains as well. Behind the overnight front, a few models 
are showing weak upslope flow across the southern foothills and 
Palmer Divide area in the mid to late morning and early afternoon.
This could be enough to trigger some shower activity in the 
southern foothills and Palmer Divide area, potentially extending 
into the southern metro. Right now, the NBM has a medium chance 
(30% to 60%) that the southern metro, Palmer, and southern 
foothills get at least a trace of snow, with very low chances 
(<10%) that Denver sees more than an inch. For the higher terrain,
snow levels will start out around 6000ft, dropping closer to 
4000ft by Saturday morning. As mentioned previously, moisture with
this event isn't particularly high, so overall accumulation's 
will be on the lower end, with liquid amounts generally around a 
few hundredths to a quarter of an inch (with potential for higher 
amounts in the Park Range). 

Behind the front, conditions are expected to be much cooler. Lows 
Saturday morning in the plains will drop into the 20s to mid 30s.
Lows for Sunday into the middle of next week are forecast to be 
in the teens to mid 20s for the plains with highs only reaching 
into the 40s each afternoon. Another shortwave trough is forecast 
to approach the area late Monday into early Tuesday, bringing
additional snow chances to the mountains.

.AVIATION /18Z TAFS through 18Z Thursday/...
Issued at 1030 AM MST Wed Nov 18 2026

There remains relatively high uncertainty on the exact locations 
of the narrow channels of stronger westerly/northwesterly winds
which could affect KAPA and KDEN over the next few hours. If 
these channels are not located directly over the site, we expect 
weak easterly winds. At this hour, one of these channels is 
located over KAPA with some gusts at or slightly above 30 kts. We 
expect winds to weaken at KAPA by about 20z. At KDEN, the 
potential remains over the next few hours for gusty winds given 
several observations 5-10 miles west of KDEN nearing 25-30 kts. We
remain most confident that KBJC will see wind gusts around 30 kts
for the next few hours due to its position closer to the higher 
terrain before weakening this afternoon.

Winds are forecast to turn clockwise and start northeast this 
afternoon. They will eventually turn to drainage and/or a modified
drainage this evening. Winds could approach 12-15 kts at times, 
but are forecast to be much weaker than this morning and early 
afternoon. For Thursday afternoon, after a brief period of vrb 
winds in the morning, winds are forecast to turn to the northeast, 
but remain under 10 kts.
