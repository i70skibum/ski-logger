.DISCUSSION /Through Saturday/...
Issued at 1203 AM MST Sun Nov 22 2026

Drier conditions are expected for today as a brief period of upper-
level ridging develops behind Saturday's storm system. Despite the 
increased subsidence, a combination of morning fog, high clouds, and 
a Denver Cyclone will aid in keeping cooler temperatures in place 
across much of the forecast area. Those on the southern side of the 
cyclone may see temperatures in the mid to upper 40s thanks to 
downsloping effects off the Palmer Divide, whereas those on its 
northern extent should expect upper 30s to low 40s where northerly 
winds will keep cooler air sliding south. Patchy freezing fog is 
expected to begin developing over the eastern plains late tonight, 
with areas of dense fog likely setting in just before sunrise. Hi-
res guidance continues to shift the corridor of thickest fog 
slightly with each run, but has continued to keep its southern most 
edge just making it into the airfield at DIA and northward along the 
I-25 corridor into Larimer County, as well as east along I-70 into 
Lincoln County. Anyone driving across the plains in the morning 
should be prepared to encounter patchy fog, with areas of 
significantly reduced visibilities to a quarter mile or less at 
times. Additionally, freezing fog can create slick surfaces which 
could lead to slippery driving conditions on top of lowered 
visibilities. A Dense Fog Advisory has been issued for Washington 
County from 1AM to 10AM, and for the northern I-25 Corridor and 
adjacent plains from Denver northward, areas along Highway 34, and I-
70 east of Denver from 4AM to 10AM. Conditions should start to 
improve by late morning. 

Synoptically, things look in decent agreement across ensemble suites 
and deterministic outputs alike, with regards to the next 
approaching shortwave expected to move across the Rockies on Monday. 
For Colorado, it looks like the axis of the upper-level ridge will 
push east by Monday morning, leaving southwesterly flow aloft in its 
wake. Afternoon high temps are forecast to remain five to ten 
degrees below normal across the lower elevations, with our northern 
mountain valleys staying right around seasonal values. Cross 
sections show a deepening moisture profile throughout the day, with 
snow showers likely developing in the mountains by the afternoon. 
Moisture still looks unimpressive with this next wave, but despite 
our lower elevations remaining dry, we should at least see some 
light mountain snowfall accumulations overnight into Tuesday 
morning (generally two inches or less), with low chances for some 
very light accumulations for portions of the Palmer Divide. 

Upper-level ridging will rebuild across the Rockies behind Monday's 
disturbance. Afternoon temperatures will gradually warm each day 
through the week, with mild and dry conditions looking likely for 
Thursday's holiday celebrations. There are enough uncertainties with 
the the evolution of our next potential storm system to not 
completely rule out light mountain snow showers through the week, 
but our lower elevations are on track to remain dry through the 
forecast period.
