.DISCUSSION /Through Thursday/...
Issued at 114 PM MST Thu Nov 26 2026

Mild Thanksgiving weather will be replaced by unsettled weather
Friday night and Saturday which will affect post holiday travel
plans.

In the meantime, tonight will feature dry conditions with only 
passing high clouds. Temperatures will settle closer to normal 
overnight lows once again. Friday is still on track to bring 
increasing winds to the Front Range and northern plains as the 
upper level ridge over us today breaks down and a stronger zonal 
flow develops across the Central Rockies. That means a return of 
gusty winds to the higher foothills and mountains, with gusts of 
45-60 mph above timberline and higher wind prone areas. 
Ingredients like stability and increasing forward shear do not 
favor significant mountain wave amplification, so we think those 
higher wind gusts should stay out of the adjacent plains. That 
said, it will still be breezy in some of the local favored 
downslope areas like Highway 93, while daytime mixing also
supports locations closer to the Wyoming border getting breezy 
with gusts of 30-35 mph in the late morning and afternoon. We'll 
introduce the first chance of snow to the northern mountains 
through the day as mid level moisture is now forecast to increase 
sufficiently for light snow showers, mainly north of Rabbit Ears 
Pass. 

The main focus shifts to Friday night and Saturday as a fast
moving shortwave drives through the Central Rockies in strong
west/northwest flow aloft. As discussed last night, models remain
in good agreement with this feature. However, most have sped up 
this disturbance by a few hours and not surprisingly as we're in 
such strong zonal flow. This means snow will start in earnest 
across the northern mountains Friday night. We'll first see this 
more significant snowfall develop in the northern tier of 
mountains where westerly orographic flow is more favorable and 
moisture is deeper, but even the I-70 Corridor should start to get
into the heavier snow after midnight tomorrow night as large 
scale QG ascent occurs ahead of the shortwave and in the left 
exit region of a 120 kt jet. Those favorable features, along with 
passage of a mid level front and brief but rather pronounced 
frontogenesis, will support snow through the day Saturday. In 
fact, it's not impossible that we see a brief period of very heavy
snow or snow squalls with the frontal passage in the late morning
to early afternoon hours. We'll continue to message avoiding 
post-Thanksgiving travel on Saturday through the high country if 
possible. The earlier start also supports more snowfall, backed up
by the latest uptick in ensemble and operational guidance. That 
suggests the northern tier of mountains get 8-14" with the I-70 
mountain corridor and Summit County now in the 5-10" range. That 
puts us on the higher end of Winter Weather Advisory Criteria. If 
trends continue, then we'll likely have to upgrade the northern 
tier of mountain zones to a Winter Storm Warning since they'll be
beneficiaries of the earlier start. 

Now, back down to the I-25 Corridor and plains...The instability 
and favorable ingredients mentioned above will also be conducive 
to showers spreading off the higher terrain through the day. With
the earlier frontal passage, we've also trimmed forecast 
temperatures with anywhere north of Denver likely not getting out 
of the 40s before the frontal passage. The entire forecast area 
will see temperatures drop with the frontal passage as it sweeps 
quickly southward across the plains. It may arrive as early as mid
morning especially as trends continue with the faster shortwave. 
And, it should be noted we expect quite the blast of northerly 
wind across all of the plains with near high wind criteria being 
met during and shortly after the frontal passage. Widespread gusts
to 40-55 mph are expected at this point, with a 10-20% chance of 
gusts >58 mph (high wind criteria) east of I-25. Now, back to 
precip...an earlier frontal arrival would mean a higher chance 
that the synoptic scale lift and shallow anticyclonic upslope 
behind the front would be able to join forces. Thus, we've bumped 
precipitation chances further for Saturday afternoon especially 
over and south of metro Denver (more favored in N-NNE flow). 
Temperatures will also be turning colder more quickly, which 
supports more snow vs rain and a growing chance (50-60%) of our 
first official measurable snow for Denver. With the strong F-gen 
and mid level instability, we can't rule out bands of heavier snow
as well with localized amounts of 1-3" or more (favoring south 
metro into the foothills west and southwest of Denver). Any 
heavier bands could also result in brief slush accumulation on 
roads, with propensity for greater impacts in the colder foothills
and Palmer Divide should the heavier snow bands develop there. 

We do expect snow to taper off fairly quickly Saturday evening as
the system is now trending faster, but some travel impacts may 
linger in the mountains into early Sunday morning due to residual
road snow. Those conditions will improve rather quickly however
with return of sunshine, so Sunday is shaping up to be a much
better post-holiday travel day than Saturday. 

Mainly dry weather will then persist into Monday and Tuesday,
although the flow appears to become zonal enough to have a chance
of light snow showers for the high country. After a chilly Sunday,
temperatures will be warming back to above normal levels both
days. 

While models still indicate a weakening trough to impact the area
by Wednesday or Thursday, the latest runs hinted at a weak closed
low structure remaining in tact and also tracking a bit further
northward. This will mean another chance of rain/snow showers for
the forecast area with temperatures cooling to near normal levels
again.

.AVIATION /00Z TAFS through 00Z Saturday/...
Issued at 440 PM MST Thu Nov 26 2026

VFR conditions are expected through the TAF period. We expect a 
BKN high-level deck through the TAF period. Winds are expected to 
turn to drainage rather quickly this evening. At KBJC, some gusty 
winds of 20-25 kts are possible overnight as flow aloft 
strengthens. We do not expect these stronger winds to make it much
further east than KBJC.

Winds on Friday remain somewhat uncertain. In general, we expect 
northwest winds of 10-15 kts with some gusts up to 20 kts 
possible. The strongest winds during the day should continue to be
at KBJC. Models remain in somewhat good agreement that 
anticyclonic flow should shift winds to the east or southeast in 
the late afternoon to early evening. The exact timing of this 
transition still differs across model guidance. A brief period of 
VRB winds is possible as this transition occurs. 

A BKN mid-level deck looks increasingly likely by Friday evening.
Drainage winds should kick in once again on Friday evening as 
clouds begin to increase ahead of the next storm system. This 
system looks to bring light snowfall chances on Saturday to all 
three TAF sites.
