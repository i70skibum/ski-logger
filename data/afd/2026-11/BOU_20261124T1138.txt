.DISCUSSION /Through Tuesday/...
Issued at 1131 AM MST Tue Nov 24 2026

With yesterday's shortwave moving off to the east and an upper-level 
ridge building behind it, conditions over the next couple of days 
will be fairly quiet weather-wise. Highs will be in the upper 
40s/low 50s along the I-25 corridor and across the plains today and 
tomorrow, with the mountain valleys seeing highs in the upper 30s to 
low 40s. Thursday will see highs in the mid 50s for the urban 
corridor and plains, along with clear skies and light winds, making 
for a rather nice, sunny Thanksgiving. Friday should then cap off 
the week as our warmest day, as highs in the urban corridor/plains 
are expected to reach into the low 60s. 

The upper-level ridge should also start to weaken on Friday, with 
the next system building to the north and west of Colorado.
West/northwest flow aloft will start to strengthen, allowing for 
stronger winds (20-30mph sustained, 40-50mph gusts) along the 
northern Front Range. There's currently some uncertainty within the 
models regarding the exact timing of the strongest winds; the NBM 
has peak wind gusts occurring early Friday morning, between 5am-8am, 
while the deterministic GFS leans towards keeping any stronger gusts 
in the afternoon, between 12pm-6pm. Regardless, the strongest winds 
will occur at the mountain peaks and higher elevations, and with 
relative humidities expected to stay above 30% across the Front 
Range & foothills, fire weather shouldn't be much of a concern.

Things get more uncertain as we head into the weekend. Some model 
runs, namely the GFS/GEFS, favor a shallower trough, with the jet 
staying north of Colorado, while the ECMWF favors a deeper trough, 
with the jet placed over western Colorado. Additionally, the last 
several runs of the GFS have an initial weak shortwave move across 
the state Saturday into Sunday, followed by a deeper trough on 
Monday, while the ECMWF resolves only one system for the Saturday-
Sunday time frame. A lot of this uncertainty comes from 
differences in how the models are handling the interactions of the
upper-level ridge over the western U.S. with both a strong system
over the eastern U.S. and a developing shortwave off the Pacific 
northwest. Ultimately, the timing of this system, and our precip 
chances over the weekend, will depend on what that low system to 
the east does, as well as how the shortwave to the northwest 
develops. Should the eastern system slow or stall over the Ohio 
Valley area, the ridge hanging over us would have nowhere to go, 
keeping things drier and warmer over the weekend. Similarly, if 
the shortwave dissipates before reaching us, as the GFS solution 
would suggest, our precip chances would be low. On the other hand,
should that shortwave to the northwest strengthen and persist, as
the ECMWF would suggest, we might be able to get some precip 
chances and cooler temperatures into the area.
